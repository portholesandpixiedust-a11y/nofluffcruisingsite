We spent 4 nights on the Disney Wish out of Port Canaveral. Watch the full review: [The Disney Wish Gave Me a Reality Check](https://youtu.be/51RbAxR7ONg).

The Wish costs more per night than almost anything else sailing out of Port Canaveral, and a 4-night Bahamian itinerary gives you fewer nights to spread that premium across than a longer sailing would. So the question isn't whether it's beautiful — it obviously is. The question is what you actually get for the money, and whether the magic is still working by the last night.

## Boarding day: the part nobody expects

The first surprise had nothing to do with the ship itself: we boarded through the MSC terminal at Port Canaveral, not Disney's own terminal. If you've booked a Wish sailing and assumed you're walking into Disney's terminal, check your documents — it isn't a given.

Two tips worth more than anything else in this section:

- **Book your kids' club slot the moment you board**, before you do anything else. Slots fill up fast, and most first-time cruisers don't realize they can (and should) do this before lunch instead of waiting until after they've explored.
- **Staterooms aren't ready until early afternoon**, so head straight to the pool deck. Mickey's Smokestack Barbecue is open and it's the easiest way to spend the gap productively instead of standing around a hallway.

One more thing worth knowing before you sail: dining rotations aren't assigned until you're actually on board — they show up in the Navigator app, not in your pre-cruise paperwork.

## The spaces worth your time — and one rule that will catch you off guard

The Disney Wish is built around theming, which means some spaces are worth planning your day around and others are just there to photograph.

**Star Wars: Hyperspace Lounge** is the most talked-about bar on the ship — worth stopping in and forming your own opinion.

**The kids' pool has a no-toys rule that almost nobody covers.** If you're used to bringing floats, puddle jumpers, or pool toys from home, leave them packed — they're not allowed in, and finding out at the gate is a frustrating way to start the day.

**Quiet Cove**, the adults-only retreat with the infinity pool and Cove Café, sits on Deck 13 — a real hike from the family decks. Disney spread the adult spaces across Decks 3, 4, and 5 rather than concentrating them in one place, which means adults see more of the ship, but it takes more effort to get there.

**The Bayou**, the Princess and the Frog–themed lounge, is worth a stop for the beignets alone.

## The cabin: does the reputation hold up?

We booked an **oceanview stateroom**, and Disney's stateroom design is consistently the line's most-praised feature. Here's how it actually held up with three people in the room:

- **The split bathroom** — the signature feature everyone hears about before they sail.
- **Storage and space.** Genuinely generous — worth testing rather than just taking on reputation.
- **The pull-out bed**, once deployed, changes how the room feels.
- **Twice-daily housekeeping** is still standard on the Wish, which is increasingly rare across the industry — a real value point worth naming on its own.
- **Room service is included 24/7**, apart from specialty beverages — one of the things that partly offsets the fare premium. Disney has only ever charged for room service on its newest ship, the Disney Adventure; every other ship in the fleet, the Wish included, still includes it.
- **The real complaint: the room ran hot, and it never got fixed.** We reported it, and it stayed hot for the rest of the sailing. Worth knowing before you book, especially in the warmer months.

## Does rotational dining actually work?

Three restaurants rotate with you across the sailing, and your serving team moves with you — a detail that surprises a lot of first-timers.

**Arendelle: A Frozen Dining Adventure** and **Worlds of Marvel** are both dinner-and-a-show format, one Frozen-themed, one interactive with screens above most tables.

**1923** is the straight, non-themed restaurant of the three — and the one with the clearest miss of the sailing: the steak salad didn't land for us.

Beyond the rotation: the churro waffle at Marceline Market was a second disappointment, Donald's Cantina covers quick service on the pool deck, and **Pirate Night dinner was not good** — which lands harder than it sounds, since Pirate Night is the marquee evening of the whole cruise.

Two more things worth knowing before you sail: there's no dinner buffet (Marceline Market is breakfast and lunch only, so every dinner is a sit-down meal or a paid specialty venue), and Disney sells drinks à la carte — there's no drink package, so if you're used to preloading one on another line, budget accordingly.

## What's actually worth doing

**Two pirate parties, not one** — most reviews lump them together, but they're distinct events. One is Mickey-focused; the other has the full band and fireworks. **If you're sailing with a toddler, prioritize the Mickey pirate party** — it played much better for us with a small one in tow than the full band-and-fireworks version.

**AquaMouse**, Disney's first attraction at sea, is worth building your day around. Our timing tip, straight from this sailing: **there was no wait at all on port days.** If your itinerary has a port stop, ride AquaMouse while most of the ship is off exploring the island — you'll walk right on.

For anyone sailing with a toddler too small for the slide, the splash pad is the real answer — that's where we spent most of Maizie's pool time. Princess meet-and-greets are worth planning around nap schedules if you've got a small one who gets a kick out of them. Disney doesn't allow recording inside the Walt Disney Theatre, so we can't show you the shows — but we can tell you the space itself is worth the time slot if a sea day leaves you looking for something to do.

## Who the Disney Wish is actually for

**Yes, we'd sail it again.** But the honest advice is: **book it if you love Disney.** This isn't the sailing to pick if you're purely chasing the best value-per-night — the short itinerary spreads a real premium across fewer nights, and there's no drink package to soften it. What you're paying for is the theming, and on this sailing, it held up.

Set against the cost, what's actually included is the stronger half of the argument. Three rotational dinners, 24/7 room service, twice-daily housekeeping, two included movie theaters, both pirate parties, the kids' clubs, and the theater shows all came with the fare — we never paid extra for a restaurant the whole trip. The premium shows up in what's *not* included, not in what's on the ship.
