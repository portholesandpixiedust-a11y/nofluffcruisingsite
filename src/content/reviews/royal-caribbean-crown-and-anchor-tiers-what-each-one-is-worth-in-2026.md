Every figure below is checked against Royal Caribbean's own current loyalty documentation and current pricing. That matters more on this topic than most, because the numbers behind the program move. Drink values, package prices and point thresholds all drift, and a loyalty guide written two years ago can be confidently wrong today. Where a widely repeated number turned out to be stale, this page says so instead of passing it along.

## How do Crown & Anchor points work?

You earn one cruise point per night in a standard cabin and two per night in a suite. Solo travelers also earn two per night in any cabin, a rule most guides bury.

A seven-night cruise in an interior, ocean view or balcony earns seven points. The same seven nights in a Junior Suite or above earns fourteen. Sail those seven nights alone and you also earn fourteen, at no cabin upcharge.

Points never expire and the clock never resets. Each tier is a running total across every Royal Caribbean cruise you have taken. Royal Caribbean credits points about two weeks after you disembark, and asks you to allow thirty days before chasing anything that looks missing.

## How many cruises does each tier take?

| Tier | Points needed | 7-night cruises, standard cabin | 7-night cruises, suite or solo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gold | 3 | 1 | 1 |
| Platinum | 30 | 5 | 3 |
| Emerald | 55 | 8 | 4 |
| Diamond | 80 | 12 | 6 |
| Diamond Plus | 175 | 25 | 13 |
| Pinnacle Club | 700 | 100 | 50 |

The first three tiers happen to anyone who cruises Royal Caribbean more than once or twice a decade. No strategy required.

Diamond is where the math turns real. Sailing one seven-night balcony cruise a year, you reach it in about eleven years. Book that same cruise as a suite and the two-points-per-night rate cuts it to roughly six. A solo cruiser in a standard cabin lands on that same six-year pace without paying suite prices.

Diamond Plus takes roughly 25 years at one standard cruise a year, or about twelve and a half in a suite or sailing solo. Pinnacle at one standard cruise a year is a full century. Even at one suite cruise every year without fail, you are looking at fifty.

Double your pace to two cruises a year and every one of those timelines halves again. Diamond lands somewhere between three and five years depending on cabin mix. Diamond Plus between six and twelve. Pinnacle around twenty-five at the suite rate. Nobody reaches the top tier on an occasional-cruiser pace. The people holding it sail several times a year, for decades, usually in higher categories.

## What are the free drinks worth?

Diamond members get four complimentary drinks a day, Diamond Plus five, Pinnacle six. Each covers a drink worth up to $14. That cap shifts outside the US, to $13 on Singapore sailings and $15 out of the UK and Australia.

Starbucks and the Bionic Bar sit outside the benefit. Everything else on the drink menu counts. Vouchers reset at 5am each morning and do not roll over, so whatever you skip is gone.

Across a seven-night cruise, that comes to as much as $392 at Diamond, $490 at Diamond Plus and $588 at Pinnacle.

## Do the free drinks beat the Deluxe Beverage Package?

Not the way they used to. This is the number worth correcting.

An older version of this comparison priced the Deluxe Beverage Package at $54 a day, which put a seven-night package around $378. Every drink tier cleared that with room to spare. That figure is stale.

Current pricing runs $65 to $120 per person per day, set dynamically by ship and sail date. Seven nights lands between $455 and $840 before gratuity. Royal Caribbean then charges 18% gratuity on the package price, which takes the same range to roughly $537 and $991. Your loyalty drinks carry no such charge.

| Tier | 7-night voucher value | vs. cheapest package, $537 all-in | vs. priciest package, $991 all-in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diamond, 4 a day | up to $392 | Falls short | Falls well short |
| Diamond Plus, 5 a day | up to $490 | Falls short | Falls well short |
| Pinnacle, 6 a day | up to $588 | Clears it | Falls well short |

Only Pinnacle beats a package, and only on the cheapest sailings in the fleet. Diamond and Diamond Plus no longer out-value buying the package on paper the way the old math suggested.

The vouchers are still a real perk. If you drink three or four a day and would never buy a package at $65 a day, they hand you several hundred dollars of drinks you were going to pay for à la carte. That is a different argument from beating the package, and it is the honest one.

## What else does status get you?

Internet is where the case rebuilds. Diamond includes one free day of Surf + Stream per cruise, Diamond Plus two days, and Pinnacle the entire sailing on one device with no daily cap. You may know this as Voom. Royal Caribbean's own benefit grid calls it Surf + Stream now.

Internet runs around $20 a day per device. Pinnacle's free internet is therefore worth about $140 on a typical seven-night cruise, sitting on top of whatever the drink vouchers cover.

Add priority boarding, lounge access, earlier cabin selection windows and faster casino comps, and the case for the top tiers stops resting on drinks. It becomes a bundle of smaller perks that add up. ## Is the Crown Lounge as crowded as people say?

Michael Bayley, Royal Caribbean's CEO, has been asked directly about tightening Diamond Plus and Pinnacle requirements to relieve crowding. He said there are no plans to.

He did acknowledge where the complaints come from. They cluster on trans-Atlantic and trans-Pacific crossings, where top-tier members concentrate. In his words: "They're often based upon certain sailings, where we do get peak numbers of Pinnacle and Diamond Plus. But 90% of the sailings, it's just not true."

That concentration makes sense once you consider who books those crossings. Longer, less beach-heavy itineraries draw repeat, status-focused cruisers rather than first-timers, which packs the lounge on the very sailings where those members already gather.

One naming note, since older guides still get it wrong. The room is no longer the Diamond Lounge. Royal Caribbean renamed it the Crown Lounge fleet-wide in 2023. Same access rules, same qualifying tiers, different name on the door.

## Can you speed up the climb?

Royal Caribbean brought back a double points promotion in July 2026, its first in years. New bookings made between 21 and 31 July earned double the usual cruise points on sailings from September 2026 through the following April. That window has closed. It is worth walking through anyway, because Royal Caribbean has used this lever before and will likely use it again.

While it ran, a seven-night cruise that normally earned seven points earned fourteen. Stacked on a suite booking, which already earns double, a single seven-night sailing reached 28 points, four times the standard base rate. A solo cruiser in a suite under that promotion could earn as much as six points per night, or 42 points across one sailing. From a standing start, that clears Platinum and lands in Emerald territory in a single cruise.

Limits applied. New bookings only, capped at two cruises per member, and trans-Atlantic and trans-Pacific sailings were excluded.

Before you plan around this, price a suite. One often costs two to three times what a standard cabin does on the same sailing. Booking one to accelerate points makes sense only if you were leaning that way already, or if the promo math on a specific sailing tips the decision. Chasing points through upgrades you would not otherwise buy can cost more than the points return. That gets truer past Diamond, where the payoff shifts toward priority access rather than dollars.

Solo travelers get the better end of this trade. The solo rate doubles your points at no cabin upcharge, on top of the solo supplement you are already paying.

Royal Caribbean's leadership has described using these promotions strategically rather than as a standing offer, so no calendar exists to plan around. Check the current Crown & Anchor page for whatever is live when you read this.

## Which Crown & Anchor tiers are worth chasing?

Gold, Platinum and Emerald happen on their own. Worth having, not worth planning around.

Diamond is the tier that rewards attention, though the case looks different after the pricing correction above. If you already cruise Royal Caribbean often, Diamond arrives rather than needing a chase. Once it does, the drinks alone will not beat a beverage package at current pricing. What Diamond delivers reliably is priority boarding, a lounge to retreat to, a free internet day and a drink allowance most moderate drinkers come out ahead on compared with paying à la carte.

Diamond Plus and Pinnacle are a different question. They take real years of dedicated cruising, or a serious stack of suite bookings and promotional windows, or roughly half the calendar if you sail alone. The payoff past Diamond runs to priority and lounge access rather than dollar value. Pinnacle's drink allowance beats a package only on the cheapest sailings, so by that point the drinks are not why anyone is climbing.

Let Diamond come to you if you already cruise regularly. Save the deliberate budget restructuring for Diamond Plus or Pinnacle, and only if status itself is the goal.

One practical step. Your current point total is sitting in your Royal Caribbean account under the Crown & Anchor section, on the app or the website. Pull it up and run it against the thresholds above. That number tells you how many nights stand between you and your next tier, which beats any generic math on this page.
