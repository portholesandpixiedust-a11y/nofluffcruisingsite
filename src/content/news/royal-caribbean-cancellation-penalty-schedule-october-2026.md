Royal Caribbean is changing North America cancellation penalties. Cruise Hive, dated 28 Sep 2026, quotes a travel-partner email sent that day. Royal Caribbean Blog, updated 28 Sep 2026, prints full penalty tables. No Royal Caribbean newsroom release was located. The email confirms the length buckets, the end of the 25% tier, and which new sailings are covered. The day-by-day percentages below are the blog tables. The partner email did not print them.

## What changed

The partner email, as Cruise Hive quotes it, says: "We're simplifying our North America cancellation penalty structure by consolidating sailing length categories and streamlining penalty tiers."

Length categories move from 1–4 nights, 5–14 nights, and 15 nights or longer to 1–4 nights, 5–9 nights, and 10 nights or longer. A 10 to 14 night cruise moves into the 10 night bucket. Cruise Hive and Royal Caribbean Blog both report that split.

The 25% cancellation tier goes away for every sailing length. Cruise Hive says 1 to 4 night cruises never had that tier. It says the old 25% window was 89 to 75 days before embarkation on 5 to 14 night cruises, and 119 to 75 days on sailings of 15 nights or longer.

Cruise Hive says the partner email did not confirm whether the other day windows, or the percentage charged in those windows, will change. The same article later says that, for longer sailings, the timeline change it can describe is removal of that 25% tier from 11 Oct 2026. Royal Caribbean Blog prints a full replacement schedule and dates it to 11 Oct 2026. Use the tables below as that blog's schedule. Use the email as confirmation of the buckets and of the removed 25% tier.

Royal Caribbean Blog says the 1 to 4 night schedule, including holiday sailings, is unchanged. It matches the 1 to 4 night schedule Cruise Hive already describes:

- 75 days or more: no charge, except a nonrefundable deposit
- 74 to 61 days: 50% of the total price
- 60 to 31 days: 75% of the total price
- 30 days or less: 100% of the total price, with no refund

Royal Caribbean Blog's 5 to 9 night table, including holiday sailings and CruiseTours:

- 90 days or more: no charge, except a nonrefundable deposit
- 89 to 61 days: 50% of the total price
- 60 to 31 days: 75% of the total price
- 30 days or less: 100% of the total price, with no refund

On that table, the old 25% window Cruise Hive places at 89 to 75 days becomes 50%, and the 50% band runs through day 61.

Royal Caribbean Blog's table for 10 nights or longer, including holiday sailings and CruiseTours:

- 120 days or more: no charge, except a nonrefundable deposit
- 119 to 61 days: 50% of the total price
- 60 to 31 days: 75% of the total price
- 30 days or less: 100% of the total price, with no refund

Royal Caribbean Blog's summary says that for cruises of 5 nights or more, the penalty jumps from 25% to 50% at 89 days. The 5 to 9 night table matches that sentence. The 10 night table starts the 50% band at 119 days. Cruise Hive places sailings of 15 nights or longer in an old 25% window that began at 119 days. It places 10 to 14 night cruises in the old middle tier, where that 25% window began at 89 days. On the blog's new table, a 10 to 14 night cruise is in the 10 night bucket, so the 50% charge starts at 119 days. Read the table for your length. Read the 89 day sentence for the 5 to 9 night band.

## Final payment

Royal Caribbean Blog says that, as of September 2026, final payment still follows cruise length. It says 1 to 4 night cruises are due no later than 75 days before embarkation. It says weeklong voyages are still due 90 days out. It says 10 to 14 night cruises move to a 120 day final payment under the new schedule. It says the old 5 to 14 night category required payment 90 days before the cruise. Cruise Hive's write-up of the partner email does not mention final payment dates.

## Third and fourth guests

Cruise Hive says the written rule for a third or fourth guest stays in place. Enforcement changes. The line will stop waiving those penalties one booking at a time. The email says: "Moving forward, the system will calculate and apply the appropriate penalty percentage to the canceled guest's cruise fare based on the booking's penalty status at the time of cancellation." Royal Caribbean Blog reports the same automatic calculation.

## Which bookings

Cruise Hive says most new bookings stay on the current penalties until 11 Oct 2026. Its summary says the rollout then covers all new bookings. The email, as Cruise Hive quotes it, says the updates apply on 11 Oct 2026 "across all new and existing deployment[s]." The bracketed s is Cruise Hive's. Cruise Hive does not print a rule for a booking you already hold.

One group is already on the new structure. The email says: "All new Alaska, Hawaii, Pacific Coastal, and Transpacific 2028 sailings opening tomorrow, September 29, 2026, will be subject to this new cancellation penalty structure." Royal Caribbean Blog names Alaska, Pacific Coastal, and Transpacific 2028 sailings that open for booking on 29 Sep 2026. It does not name Hawaii in that sentence. Hawaii is in the Cruise Hive quotation of the email.

Cruise Hive says this note covers North America sailings. Penalties for Europe and other regions may differ.

## What you should do

If you book a North America cruise on or after 11 Oct 2026, read the penalty clock for your length before you pay the deposit. If you book an Alaska, Hawaii, Pacific Coastal, or Transpacific 2028 sailing that opens on 29 Sep 2026, use the new buckets now. On a 10 to 14 night cruise, budget the 10 night table and the 120 day final payment Royal Caribbean Blog prints. If you might drop a third or fourth person, plan on the system charging the penalty. If your booking is already ticketed, wait until Royal Caribbean or your advisor says that reservation moved. The partner email, via Cruise Hive, does not say that it did.
