If an email asks you to authenticate a card, finish check-in, or stop a cancellation, and it already knows your Norwegian booking, do not click it.

[Cruise Hive](https://www.cruisehive.com/norwegian-cruise-line-investigating-scam-emails-that-know-booking-details/220373), Calvin Musquez, 12 Oct 2026, says Norwegian Cruise Line is investigating fraudulent emails that include real reservation details. [Nautical Flock](https://www.nauticalflock.com/news/norwegian-cruise-line-phishing-scam-emails-real-booking-details-october-2026), 13 Oct 2026, quotes a guest advisory posted on NCL.com. A standalone newsroom URL for that notice was not found on 13 Oct.

Norwegian has not said how the booking details got out. Neither report confirms a data breach. This post does not call it one.

## What do the emails contain?

Cruise Hive says passengers began posting the messages on 10 Oct 2026. The reported emails use a real name, a real ship, a real sail date, and a real booking number. Some say the booking could be cancelled if you do not act quickly.

One message shared on Reddit, and quoted by Cruise Hive, is aimed at a Norwegian Getaway reservation. The fraudulent email says Norwegian requires a single security authentication for the card on file, "to safeguard your booking and provide a smooth check-in." It then says, "This is a security measure, not a request for payment." That wording is from the fraudulent email. Norwegian's advisory, quoted below, does not use it.

Cruise Hive also reports a Norwegian Encore passenger whose email included the amount paid, and a Norwegian Getaway passenger who received at least six messages with accurate booking and personal information.

Nautical Flock adds reports of family names, stateroom details, the fare paid, and a note that the cruise was paid in full. It says one Encore guest, booked Vancouver to Los Angeles, called Norwegian and was told the email was fake.

Accurate details do not make the link safe. The ask is still a card number, or a check-in form, on a site you did not open yourself.

## What has Norwegian said?

Cruise Hive quotes a letter to travel advisors: "We are aware of reports of fraudulent emails falsely claiming to be from Norwegian Cruise Line being sent to guests and are actively investigating the matter."

The same article says Norwegian posted a notice on its website, sent that letter to advisors, and will share the warning with guests who depart within the next 30 days. It says official mail ends in @ncl.com or @nclcorp.com. It says not to click links, not to reply, and not to give personal or payment information.

Nautical Flock quotes the on-site advisory this way. Norwegian is investigating reports of fraudulent emails claiming to be from the line. Do not click links, respond, or provide personal or payment information. Norwegian emails come from @ncl.com, @e.ncl.com, or @nclcorp.com. For a reservation question, contact your travel advisor or call 866-234-7350. Outside the United States, use [ncl.com/contact-us](https://www.ncl.com/contact-us).

The advisor letter, as Cruise Hive prints it, names two domains. The website advisory, as Nautical Flock prints it, also names @e.ncl.com. Treat a sender outside those three as suspect. Cruise Hive also says a from-address can be spoofed. A familiar domain is not a reason to type a card into a link from the message.

## What should you do?

Type ncl.com yourself, or open the Norwegian app you already use. Do not use the button in the email. If you need a person, use your travel advisor or the number in the advisory Nautical Flock quotes.

If you already entered a card on one of these pages, call your bank and ask them to block it. Nautical Flock also says to change the password on your Norwegian account, and on any account that shares that password.

A paid-in-full booking does not need a fresh card check through an outside link to avoid cancellation. That threat is in the passenger reports. It is not in the advisory text quoted above.

## Earlier warnings, briefly

This email wave is new. The pattern of someone pretending to be the line is not.

[Cruise Hive on 23 May 2025](https://www.cruisehive.com/norwegian-cruise-line-warns-cruise-guests-of-costly-scam/175028), Catie Kovelman, reported a phone scam. A Norwegian guest-services employee said on Reddit that a caller from a 301 area code posed as the air department, offered flights for a large party, and left the guest out close to $20,000 across two incidents. That figure is the employee's account in that article. It is a phone case from 2025, not a total for these emails.

Cruise Hive's 12 Oct 2026 article also notes that Virgin Voyages warned guests in February 2025 about scam calls and fake offers. That is background. The step today is the email in your inbox.

## What this means for you

Do not click, reply, or pay from one of these messages, even when your name, ship, date, and booking number are right. Check the full sender address against @ncl.com, @e.ncl.com, and @nclcorp.com, then still open your reservation by typing ncl.com yourself. If you sail in the next 30 days, watch for Norwegian's own warning, and ignore any link that asks for a card. If you already submitted a card, call the bank. Facts above follow Cruise Hive on 12 Oct 2026 and Nautical Flock on 13 Oct 2026. The May 2025 phone case follows Cruise Hive's 23 May 2025 report. Norwegian has not confirmed a breach.
