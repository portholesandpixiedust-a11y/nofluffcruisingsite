Norwegian Joy docked late in Sitka on 17 Sep 2026. Cruise Hive and Alaska Public Media both reported the cause the next day: a deceased juvenile male fin whale on the ship's bulbous bow.

## What happened at the pier

U.S. Coast Guard, NOAA, and the Alaska Marine Mammal Stranding Network handled removal. That work delayed arrival by more than two hours. Norwegian kept the ship in Sitka about an hour longer than planned to give you more shore time after the late dock.

For passengers, the practical hit was a late start ashore. Tours with tight morning start times were the first to feel it. If your operator required a 8am meetup, ask whether they held the departure or issued a partial refund.

## What investigators still do not know

Alaska Public Media reported that NOAA had not determined whether the ship struck a living whale or hit an already dead animal. A necropsy was still pending in mid-September reporting. Until that work finishes, do not treat either explanation as settled.

Fin whales are an endangered species. That status is why federal and state responders moved carefully around documentation and removal. Ships in these waters already operate under speed and reporting rules aimed at reducing whale risk.

## Keeping the report restrained

This post sticks to passenger timing and the official response. Graphic detail does not help you plan a port day. Both Cruise Hive and Alaska Public Media are Tier 2 sources here, and they align on the late arrival and the removal timeline.

Norwegian's public messaging in those reports focused on the delay and the extra hour alongside, not on speculation about fault.

## What you should do

If you were on that Sitka call, check any prepaid tour vouchers for missed-start policies.
