Independence of the Seas is not going to Bermuda on the cruise that leaves Cape Liberty on Saturday 24 Oct 2026. [Cruise Hive](https://www.cruisehive.com/storm-forces-independence-of-the-seas-to-abandon-bermuda-for-canada/221682) and [Royal Caribbean Blog](https://www.royalcaribbeanblog.com/2026/10/23/royal-caribbean-reroutes-another-warm-weather-cruise-canada-after-threatening-weather) both print an email that sends the ship to Saint John, New Brunswick, for an overnight stay.

The [public itinerary page](https://www.royalcaribbean.com/cruises/itinerary/5-night-bermuda-from-cape-on-independence/ID05BYE-1681436210?country=USA&currency=USD&sail-date=2026-10-24) still said Bermuda when it was read on 23 Oct. Use the email in your booking until that page changes. Neither article links a Royal Caribbean newsroom post.

The photo on this page is the lead photo on the Cruise Hive story, Independence of the Seas departing New Jersey, credited to Mariusz Lopusiewicz. The caption does not date it to Saturday.

## What the email says

Cruise Hive says the note went out late on Thursday 22 Oct, less than 48 hours before you board.

Royal Caribbean Blog quotes this decision: the weather's impact makes it hard to provide you with a safe and comfortable journey to Bermuda, so after exploring all of the options the line decided to sail to Saint John, New Brunswick, Canada, for an overnight stay instead.

Cruise Hive prints the same decision. In the weather sentence, that article prints "make is" where the blog prints "makes it." The rest of the decision matches. This post follows the blog's spelling and flags the other one.

The email quotes in both articles say the line has been monitoring inclement weather off the East Coast with its meteorology team. They do not name a storm. Royal Caribbean Blog's lede calls it Subtropical Storm Josephine. The weather service name is below.

Cruise Hive's original Bermuda call was King's Wharf at the Royal Naval Dockyard, 9:00 a.m. Monday 26 Oct until 5:00 p.m. Tuesday 27 Oct. The public page, read 23 Oct, still lists that window, a 3:00 p.m. Saturday departure, and a 7:00 a.m. arrival on Thursday 29 Oct. Those clocks are the page that has not been updated. They are not a new instruction in the email.

Both articles call the ship a 3,858-passenger ship. That count is not on the itinerary page checked here.

The blog says the email recommends light sweaters and layers, and that the forecast calls for daytime temperatures in the mid-50s while you are in port. It also points you to the New Brunswick Museum and the Reversing Rapids, where the Saint John River meets the Bay of Fundy. Those lines are the blog's report of the email. Cruise Hive does not print them.

A different Saint John call is already on this site. [Vision of the Seas drops Bermuda, and that email says you stay aboard](/news/vision-of-the-seas-drops-bermuda-saint-john-stay-aboard-october-2026/). That is the 17 Oct Baltimore cruise. The credit on that sailing was a different table, and that email said you could not get off. This Independence email, as the blog prints it, talks about going ashore.

The September weather delay on this ship is also a different cruise. [Independence of the Seas held at Cape Liberty in the nor'easter](/news/independence-of-the-seas-noreaster-cape-liberty-bermuda-day-call/) is the 26 Sep sailing.

## The credit

Both articles print the same refundable onboard credit. It is per stateroom for the first and second guests.

| Stateroom | Credit for the first two guests |
|---|---|
| Inside | $150 |
| Outside | $200 |
| Balcony | $300 |
| Suite | $500 |
| Each guest after the first two | $50 |

Royal Caribbean Blog's example is four people in a balcony, $400. That is $300 plus $50 plus $50. The articles call the credit refundable. Prepaid shore excursions bought through Royal Caribbean for Bermuda are cancelled and refunded to the onboard account. Royal Caribbean Blog names Royal Naval Dockyard. Credit left at the end of the sailing goes back to the card on that account. The quotes do not print a fare refund.

## Canada, and the forecast

Royal Caribbean Blog quotes the email on documents. You can travel with a passport book or card valid for at least 6 months after the end of the sailing. The email says it highly recommends that. The article also says a U.S. birth certificate and a valid government ID can be used, and that some nationalities may need a visa and may not be able to board. Cruise Hive does not print those lines. Open the travel link in your own email before you leave for the pier.

The [Ocean Prediction Center product](https://ocean.weather.gov/shtml/NFDOFFNT2.php) issued at 10:43 a.m. EDT on Friday 23 Oct says Post-Tropical Cyclone Josephine was southeast of Nova Scotia that morning and would move farther east during the day. It tells mariners to avoid the system and to keep reading the forecasts. A gale warning in that product covers Cape Fear to 31N to 1000 fm. Saturday night there is 30 to 40 knots and seas of 12 to 19 feet. Sunday is 30 to 40 knots and seas of 12 to 20 feet. That zone is one box in a larger forecast. This post does not put the ship inside it. Cruise Hive's single set of wind and sea numbers is that article's summary.

## What this means if you board on Saturday

Pack for Saint John, not a beach day in Bermuda. The hours to plan around are Monday morning through 2:30 p.m. Tuesday, with Cruise Hive's "approximately" on the Monday arrival. Read the Canada line in the email, including a visa if your nationality needs one.

Match the credit to the cabin. It is refundable, in both reports, and it is not a fare refund. If you bought a Bermuda tour through Royal Caribbean, look for that money on the onboard account.

If the website still shows Royal Naval Dockyard, that was the state of the public page on 23 Oct. The email is the change.
