Havila is pausing investments for Norway's next coastal contract. The company posted two releases on 27 Oct 2026. The [stock-exchange note](https://investor.havilavoyages.com/pressreleases/havila-kystruten-as-potential-implications-of-proposed-framework-for-the-next-co) says the current Bergen-Kirkenes contract runs through 2030 and is not changed. The [news release](https://investor.havilavoyages.com/pressreleases/investments-on-hold-uncertainty-over-new-coastal-route-agreement) the same day, and [Cruise Industry News](https://cruiseindustrynews.com/cruise-news/2026/10/havila-pauses-investments-over-proposed-shorter-coastal-contract/) the same day, warn that the coast could lose the route after 2035.

The photo on this page is the lead image on the Cruise Industry News story. The file name is Havila-Capella-Mjolstadnese.jpg. The article does not name a photographer. Neither Havila release says this pause applies only to Havila Capella. The stock-exchange page shows a logo, so this post uses the Cruise Industry News file.

## What Oslo put on the table

Both Havila pages describe the same budget idea. The next contract for the Bergen-Kirkenes coastal route, after 2030, would run four or five years. The current agreements run 10 years.

The stock-exchange release also says the proposal still wants daily calls, more room for cargo, and more room for private cars. Climate rules beyond the law would be set only where the cost is justified. The news release objects to that climate line. Cruise Industry News reports the objection.

## What Havila stopped spending on

The two company pages do not describe the same pause.

The stock-exchange release says Havila is reassessing investments for the next contract. The specific hold is narrower. Car-deck work and the other adaptations for that next contract were going to happen during scheduled maintenance. On ships where that work has not started, it is now on hold. The release does not say a ship already in the yard has to undo the work.

The news release says planned investments for the next contract period are on hold, including the car modifications at every port. It does not print the not-yet-started limit. Bent Martini, the chief executive, says a four-to-five-year contract is no basis for new ships or major technology, so some investments wait. Cruise Industry News follows that wider wording and quotes the same line. It does not print the ship-by-ship limit.

Martini also says, in the news release, that Havila can still operate. The ships were built so they can work off the coastal route. The stock-exchange release says the same thing without the quote: the vessels were designed for use outside the route, and a short contract would push growth into other markets.

The news release says Havila and Hurtigruten showed alternative fuels in fall 2025, and that Havila's ships already emit less CO2 than the contract requires. Those two sentences are not in the stock-exchange release. Cruise Industry News does not print them.

Per Sævik, the founder, is in the news release and in Cruise Industry News. He says a contract this short gives no reason to invest, and the communities that need the daily call are the ones who lose. The stock-exchange release does not quote him.

## What this means if you are booked, or if you were waiting on a newer ship

A cruise you hold under the contract that runs through 2030 is not cancelled by these pages. Only the stock-exchange release says that in a direct sentence. It also prints no refund, because it does not take the sailing away. The news release and Cruise Industry News do not tell you to rebook.

What changed is the next contract, the one after 2030. Parliament still has to vote. Havila's own pages put that vote in December 2026. Until then, the company is not spending on the next-contract upgrades in the way it had planned. If you wanted car space on a ship that had not started that work, the stock-exchange release says that modification is on hold. If you wanted the 2028 climate-neutral target to stay a promise, that same release says the target will be reassessed. The news release still states the biogas aim and then says the short contract may not fund it.

The 2035 line is a warning, not a closed route. It is in the news release and in Cruise Industry News. It is not in the stock-exchange release. The photo of Havila Capella is the Cruise Industry News file. It is not a list of the ships covered by the pause.
