Global Ports Holding signed a 30-year concession for St. Vincent Cruise Port in Kingstown. The [company release](https://www.globalportsholding.com/whats-new-insights/global-ports-holding-expands-caribbean-network-with-st-vincent-cruise-port/), dated 28 Oct 2026, says the signing was 26 Oct 2026 at Kingstown Cruise Ship Berth. Prime Minister Dr. Godwin Friday was there. [Cruise Industry News](https://cruiseindustrynews.com/cruise-news/2026/10/gph-secures-30-year-concession-for-st-vincent-cruise-port/), 28 Oct, and [Seatrade Cruise News](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/ports-destinations/st-vincent-ties-the-knot-with-global-ports-holding), Anne Kalosh, the same day, report that release.

The photo is the lead image on the Global Ports page. The page does not name a photographer. The banner in the frame reads Signing Ceremony. This post does not name the people standing in the line.

Seatrade uses a different Global Ports photo. Its caption says Friday, on the left, shakes hands with Mehmet Kutman. This post does not use that crop.

## What was signed?

The release says Global Ports Holding, which calls itself the world's largest cruise port operator, will develop and operate the port. The other party is the Government of St. Vincent and the Grenadines. The term is 30 years.

The release says the concession follows a memorandum of understanding from June 2026. It does not print the June day. Cruise Industry News prints June 2026 as well. Seatrade calls it the memorandum and does not print the month in the body used here.

## When does the company take over?

The release says operations are expected in the first quarter of 2027. That start depends on conditions precedent in the agreement. Cruise Industry News prints the same caveat. Seatrade prints the quarter and does not print the words conditions precedent.

Use the release. Early 2027 is the target. It is not a date you can put on a calendar yet. None of these pages prints a day inside that quarter.

## What is supposed to change?

The release describes a phased investment. The work is to modernize the port, expand berthing so larger cruise ships can call, and improve the visit once you are ashore. Global Ports also says it will work with the government and local people on shore excursions and on business beyond the pier.

The release expects the port to welcome over 250,000 cruise passengers in the 2026/27 season. Seatrade says more than 250,000. Those two phrases are the same scale. The release does not print a dollar total. It does not print a new berth count. It does not print the length of ship that can dock, or the season when that larger ship arrives.

Mehmet Kutman, chairman and chief executive, says the aim is a modern, sustainable port whose value reaches past the gate. Mike Maura, Jr., regional director for the Americas, points to Global Ports work in San Juan and Nassau and says the focus in St. Vincent is the guest experience and local business. Seatrade prints both quotes. Cruise Industry News prints Kutman and does not print Maura in the text read here.

## What did the prime minister say?

The Global Ports release names Friday at the signing. It does not quote him.

The [St. Vincent Tribune](https://stvincenttribune.com/pm-dr-friday-demands-results-as-svg-signs-gph-cruise-deal/), Val Matthias, updated 9:27 p.m. on Monday 26 Oct 2026, does. Friday said the partnership should create opportunities for all Vincentians. He said MIMIT and Global Ports are his partners, and that his clients are the people of St. Vincent and the Grenadines. The Tribune does not spell out MIMIT. This post does not invent the expansion.

He said ships have become significantly larger, and that the country does not intend to be left behind. The Tribune says the government acknowledges that the current cruise facility was built more than 26 years ago. That age is the Tribune's. It is not in the 28 Oct release.

The Tribune also says Friday named taxi drivers, tour operators, vendors, craft producers, restaurant owners, entertainers, farmers, fishers, and truckers as people who could see more work. That list is his remarks as the Tribune reports them. It is not a hiring notice.

## What this means for you

If you have a St. Vincent call this season, this signing does not move your ship. The release does not cancel a call, change a pier time, or print a new terminal map. The 250,000 figure is the season the company expects, not a count of people already ashore.

If you are booking a later Southern Caribbean cruise and you care about a bigger ship in Kingstown, the release says larger ships are the point of the berth work. It does not say when that berth opens. The handover itself is early 2027 only if the conditions in the contract are met. Check the port page again before you plan a tour around a ship that cannot dock there yet.
