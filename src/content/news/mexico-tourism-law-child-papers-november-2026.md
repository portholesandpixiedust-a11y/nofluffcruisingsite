Mexico published a rule that can stop a tourism service when an adult is traveling with a child and cannot show official papers. The decree is in the [Diario Oficial de la Federación](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/5803838). The record dates it 13 Nov 2026. The decree says it takes effect the day after publication, which is 14 Nov 2026.

The photo is the lead image on [Royal Caribbean Blog's 16 Nov article](https://www.royalcaribbeanblog.com/2026/11/16/mexico-new-family-travel-rule). The file name is mariner-of-the-seas-docked-cozumel.jpg. The hull lettering reads MARINER OF THE SEAS. The page, as fetched, does not print a photographer. The decree does not name that ship.

## What the decree requires

The Spanish text adds two duties to article 58 of the Ley General de Turismo, for prestadores de servicios turísticos, tourism service providers.

[Travel Market Report](https://www.travelmarketreport.com/canada/destinations/articles/mexico-formalizes-proof-of-guardianship-law-for-hotels-but-questions-remain), Michael Pihach, 16 Nov, prints this English reading. Providers must require tourists travelling with minors to show documentation of family relationship, parental authority, legal guardianship, or custody. Where it applies, a person must show official documents that they are of legal age. If the documents are not provided, the service is denied, and the provider notifies investigating authorities of the possible commission of a crime.

The same decree tells providers to follow preventive protocols that the competent authorities define. It does not print those protocols.

The decree does not name a birth certificate, a consent letter, a cruise line, a gangway, or a port. Travel Market Report says the reform is nationwide. It is not limited to one state.

## What the cruise writeup adds

[Royal Caribbean Blog](https://www.royalcaribbeanblog.com/2026/11/16/mexico-new-family-travel-rule), Elizabeth Wright, updated 16 Nov, says the law does not appear to change the requirements for cruise passengers walking off the ship. It says beach clubs, resorts, and shore excursions may ask for papers. That gangway sentence is the blog's reading. It is not a sentence in the decree. No cruise line notice is quoted on that page.

The blog says authorities have not published a list of accepted documents. Its own suggested set includes passports, a long-form birth certificate, custody papers where they apply, and a notarized consent letter for some adults who are not the legal guardian. Travel Market Report prints a shorter set from advisor Lori Gold and labels it precautionary. The two lists are not the same, and neither one is in the decree. This post does not merge them into a required packet.

Travel Market Report says hotel brands and tour operators it contacted could not say which papers they will take. Gold's line in that piece is that many resorts may not have a procedure yet.

## If you already hold a Mexico call

If you are booked on a cruise that calls in Mexico and you are traveling with a minor, the decree is aimed at the provider of the tourism service, not at a line-by-line pier rule. Ask the hotel, beach club, or shore operator on your booking which documents it wants, and whether a copy is enough. The decree does not answer that. It also does not say a ship will refuse you the gangway.

The phone and the address on the Diario Oficial record are for the gazette, Río Amazonas No. 62, Mexico City, and dof@segob.gob.mx. They are not a cruise-line help desk.
