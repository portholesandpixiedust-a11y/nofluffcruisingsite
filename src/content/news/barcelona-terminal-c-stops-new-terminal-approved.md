If your Barcelona documents still say Terminal C, check them before you go to the pier. The port says that building stops.

[Port of Barcelona](https://www.portdebarcelona.cat/en/communication/news/port-barcelona-takes-steps-reorganise-cruise-terminals-adossat-wharf), in an article dated 30 Oct 2026, says its Management Board revised the concession held by Creuers del Port de Barcelona. Operations in the current Terminal C cease on 1 Nov. The building is then emptied and demolished.

[Cruise & Ferry](https://www.cruiseandferry.net/articles/barcelona-cruise-port-to-develop-new-104m-sustainable-cruise-terminal), Laura Hyde, 3 Nov 2026, and [Cruise Industry News](https://cruiseindustrynews.com/cruise-news/2026/11/port-of-barcelona-okays-new-cruise-terminal-extends-concession-to-2050/) the same day, report the new terminal. Neither of those pages prints the 1 Nov stop. The closure line is the port's.

The photo is the rendering on the Cruise & Ferry page. The visible line reads: the new cruise terminal will feature solar panels and shore power capability. The credit is GPH/Barcelona Cruise Port. The page does not date the drawing.

## What closes, and what gets built

The port says the new Terminal C stands on the same site. It is 11,253 square metres. It is planned with photovoltaic generation and shore power, so a ship can use the grid at the berth. Cruise & Ferry and Cruise Industry News print the same size and the same shore-power plan.

The port also moves the roundabout into Adossat wharf, builds the roads and intermodal areas, and adds a passenger walkway. It says the terminal will be public.

The spend on the port page is €104.5 million. More than €50 million of that is the building. Cruise & Ferry writes the same euros and adds $116.8 million and $55.8 million. Those dollars are that page's conversion. The port page does not print them. Cruise Industry News stays with the euros.

No page here names a ship. No page prints a fare, a cancelled call, or a temporary terminal for this week.

## The concession, and the buildings around it

The port says the old concession ran to 2030, with an option to 2033. The new Terminal C concession ends in 2050, with an option to 2053. Cruise & Ferry and Cruise Industry News print 2050 and 2053. They do not print 2030.

The port says Terminals A and B come down once the new Terminal C is in service. That demolition is described as a step before work on a 660-metre stretch of Adossat wharf that is more than 70 years old. The wharf job is due to be tendered in the coming months, to start late 2029, and to take 24 months. The budget printed for that job is €42 million.

Late 2029 is the wharf project. It is not printed as the day the new terminal opens. No page here gives the new terminal an opening date.

The port ties the whole move to a July 2025 agreement with Barcelona City Council. The aim in that article is fewer cruise terminals on the wharf, from seven to five. The trade pages do not print the seven-to-five line.

## What to do if you are booked through Barcelona

If the ticket or the transfer says Terminal C, ask the cruise line which door is open on your date. The port article does not give you a substitute terminal.

The same page prints a general port number, +34 93 298 60 00, and sau@portdebarcelona.cat. That is the contact on the article. It is not labeled as a cruise-day hotline.

Shore power is part of the new building. Nothing on these pages says a ship at Barcelona this week can plug in.
