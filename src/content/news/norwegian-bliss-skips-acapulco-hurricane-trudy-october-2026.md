Acapulco is off the week. Saturday is a sea day, and nobody has named a pier to replace it.

[Cruise Hive](https://www.cruisehive.com/norwegian-bliss-cancels-acapulco-call-as-hurricane-trudy-strengthens/221105), Melissa Mayntz, 16 Oct 2026, prints a letter that went to staterooms on Norwegian Bliss. [The Travel](https://www.thetravel.com/norwegian-cruise-line-last-minute-cancellation-acapulco-mexico-hurricane-trudy/), Carla Vianna, 17 Oct 2026 at 11:38 a.m. EDT, quotes the same notice from a copy uploaded to Facebook. No Norwegian newsroom post for the letter was found. The two articles are the record.

The sentence they share:

> Due to the projected path of Hurricane Trudy, we have made the decision to adjust our route to maintain a safe distance from the storm and avoid unfavorable weather conditions.

Cruise Hive quotes the next line too. The ship will be unable to visit Acapulco, Mexico, on 17 Oct 2026, as scheduled. The Travel stops at the first sentence. The cancellation is in both reports.

## Which cruise, and which day

This is a 19-night repositioning. It left Los Angeles on Tuesday 13 Oct 2026. Both articles say it is due in New York on Sunday 1 Nov. Cabo San Lucas, on Thursday 15 Oct, already happened. Acapulco was the next port.

Cruise Hive prints the lost window as 7:30 a.m. to 6 p.m. on Saturday. The Travel says the ship was due in the morning and does not print those clocks. The hours in this post are Cruise Hive's.

Saturday replaces that call with a day at sea. Cruise Hive says no alternative port was available, and that no other change is confirmed. The Travel says an updated itinerary showed no additional changes. Friday 16 Oct and Sunday 18 Oct were already sea days in The Travel's account, so the skip makes three sea days in a row, Friday through Sunday.

The two articles do not agree on when you were told. The Travel dates the notice to Thursday 15 Oct. Cruise Hive says the letter went to the rooms once the decision was confirmed, and it does not print that Thursday date. Plan from the cancellation, not from which day the paper slid under the door.

## What is still on the route

The quoted letter does not list the later ports. Cruise Hive describes them as Guatemala, Costa Rica, Panama, Colombia, Jamaica, and Florida, plus a Panama Canal transit on Saturday 24 Oct, then New York on Sunday 1 Nov. It says the canal booking is a reason not to lose a day off Mexico.

The Travel prints a day-by-day table. Treat the table as that article's itinerary. It matches Cruise Hive on the countries, the canal date, and New York.

## The tours

An Acapulco excursion you bought through Norwegian is cancelled. Cruise Hive says the refund posts to the onboard account, and it points questions to the Shore Excursion Desk on Deck 6. The Travel calls it a monetary credit to that same account. Neither article says the tour money goes straight back to your card.

A tour you booked with someone else is outside that refund. Call the operator. Acapulco is not going to happen from this ship on Saturday, on the facts these two reports share.

Neither article prints a fare refund or a flat onboard credit for the missed port. The tour refund is the money they describe.

Cruise Hive's passenger figure is 4,004. The Travel says Norwegian lists 4,010 at double occupancy, and that the line has not said how many people are aboard. The counts disagree. This post does not use either one.

## Where the storm stood

Cruise Hive's storm box is a Friday-morning reading. It puts Trudy 235 miles southwest of Acapulco, at 120 mph, a Category 3, with strengthening expected through Saturday. That wind and that category match National Hurricane Center Advisory 12, issued at 3:00 a.m. CST on Friday 16 Oct. The 235-mile distance is in the article. It is not repeated from the bulletin text used here. Advisory 12 also said Trudy was forecast to remain a major hurricane at its closest approach. That sentence did not survive the afternoon.

Advisory 14, at 3:00 p.m. CST the same Friday, came from a Hurricane Hunter flight. It called Trudy a Category 1, with winds near 85 mph, about 225 miles southwest of Acapulco, moving north-northwest near 5 mph. A hurricane warning ran from Manzanillo north to Cabo Corrientes. A tropical storm warning ran from Manzanillo south to Zihuatanejo. The bulletin does not put Acapulco on that list.

Advisory 17, at 9:00 a.m. CST on Saturday 17 Oct, put the center about 265 miles west of Acapulco and about 170 miles south of Manzanillo. Winds were near 90 mph. The storm was moving northwest near 10 mph, with some strengthening still possible Saturday and weakening expected to begin Sunday. The center was forecast to pass near the west-central coast Saturday night into Sunday. The hurricane warning was still Manzanillo to Cabo Corrientes. The bulletin does not name Bliss.

The Travel calls Trudy a Category 1 about 255 miles west of Acapulco and does not name an advisory number. Saturday morning's bulletin is 265 miles and 90 mph, which is still Category 1. Use Advisory 17 for the Saturday position. The Friday-morning Category 3 line is the old one.

Cruise Hive also says AIS showed Bliss already farther out to sea than the coastal route it had planned. That is the article's tracking check. This post did not open an AIS site.

[Brilliant Lady's 17 Oct sailing](/news/brilliant-lady-hurricane-trudy-california-october-17-2026/) is a different ship, leaving Los Angeles today, and it swapped the whole Mexican Riviera for California. It is not this repositioning.

## What this means for you

If you are on Bliss, Saturday is a sea day. Keep Monday for Guatemala unless the app says otherwise, and keep Saturday 24 Oct for the canal unless Norwegian sends a new letter. Check the onboard account for an Acapulco tour you bought from the line, or ask on Deck 6. Call any tour you arranged yourself. Leave a fare refund out of the budget. Facts above follow Cruise Hive on 16 Oct 2026, The Travel on 17 Oct 2026, and National Hurricane Center Advisories 12, 14, and 17.
