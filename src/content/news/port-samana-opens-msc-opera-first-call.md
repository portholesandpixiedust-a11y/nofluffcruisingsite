Port Samaná is open, and MSC Opera was the first cruise ship alongside. [Seatrade Cruise News](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/ports-destinations/-90m-port-saman-opens-with-msc-opera-first-alongside), Anne Kalosh, dates the story 27 Nov 2026. President Luis Abinader and Tourism Minister David Collado were at the ribbon cutting. The article does not print a clock, and it does not print the calendar day of the call.

The image on this page is the lead file on that Seatrade story, Samana-ribbon-cutting.jpg. The caption credits the photo to ITM GROUP. It names David Collado at the center, in blue, President Luis Abinader to his right, and ITM Group chief executive Mauricio Hamui next. It says MSC Opera is at the dock. The frame is a ribbon cutting in front of a sign that reads Port Samaná, with a white cruise ship behind the group. The bow name is readable.

## What opened

Seatrade's headline puts the project at $90 million. The port is a public-private project of Mexico's ITM Group and the Dominican Republic. It is the former Port Duarte, in Arroyo Barril. ITM has a 33-year operating lease. The pier has two berths. Seatrade calls an adventure park and Xamana Village part of the blueprint. It does not say those venues were open for the first call.

[ITM's release](https://itmgroup.mx/msc-opera-marca-un-nuevo-hito-con-su-primer-arribo-a-port-samana/), published 25 Nov 2026, says the first call brought more than 2,200 cruise guests. It says President Luis Abinader inaugurated the port, with first lady Raquel Arbaje. ITM calls this its third port in the Dominican Republic. Seatrade names the other two as Cabo Rojo and Taino Bay. The ITM page spells the name Port Samana. Seatrade uses Samaná. The ITM body does not print a date.

[Cibao Noticias](https://www.cibaonoticias.com/port-samana-inicia-operaciones-arroyo-barril-msc-opera/) on 24 Nov 2026 says the terminal began operations that day with MSC Opera and about 2,000 passengers. Seatrade and the ITM release do not print 24 Nov. The two guest counts do not match. This post does not pick one.

## The 2027 number is not one number

Seatrade prints a projection of 250,000 passengers in 2027, 540,000 in 2028, and 1 million by 2030. Cibao Noticias says the port authority estimated about 230,000 for 2027, and that President Abinader mentioned 250,000 at the opening. The 250,000 line in Seatrade is Seatrade's figure. It is not a settled count.

A separate line in Seatrade is easy to mix up with those. Hamui said ITM expects 2.7 million cruisers in 2028 across Port Samaná, Taino Bay, and Cabo Rojo together. That is three ports, not the 540,000 figure for Samaná alone.

## If you are on MSC Opera from La Romana

[Cruise Industry News on 12 Nov](https://cruiseindustrynews.com/cruise-news/2026/11/msc-opera-sets-sail-for-caribbean-after-cruise-cancellation/) says MSC Opera was due in La Romana on 16 Nov 2026 and would then start seven-night cruises roundtrip from La Romana. It calls Opera the first MSC ship to sail year-round in the Southern Caribbean. It names Bridgetown, Tortola, Fort-de-France, and Philipsburg. It does not say weekly.

[The 26 Nov note](https://cruiseindustrynews.com/cruise-news/2026/11/msc-showcases-year-round-program-from-la-romana/) says the year-round La Romana program departs weekly, as seven nights or a 14-night back-to-back, with Martinique, Antigua and Barbuda, and the British Virgin Islands, plus Catalina Sugar Beach on each departure. The body does not print a ship name. It calls the ship Lirica class. The lead file is MSC-Opera.jpg, and the alt text is MSC Opera. The article does not name a photographer. This post does not treat the file name as a sentence that names the ship.

No Cruise Industry News story on the Port Samaná opening turned up on 27 Nov. This post does not add calls by other lines. The [5 Nov post](/news/msc-opera-grand-voyage-cancelled-la-romana-november-2026/) is the cancelled crossing, not this port.
