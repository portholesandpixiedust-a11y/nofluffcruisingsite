Villa Vie Residences suspended operations in Cambodia on 27 Oct 2026. The ship is the Villa Vie Odyssey. The [company release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/villa-vie-residences-suspends-operations-in-cambodia-as-it-pursues-restructuring-and-recapitalization-302920622.html), Fort Lauderdale, 27 Oct 2026 at 06:59 ET, is the statement. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/us-cambodia-cruise-ship-fuel-88fa620415d8e490cbe5a25ca5cb0691), Sopheng Cheang, 28 Oct, says the ship is docked at Sihanoukville. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/28/villa-vie-odyssey-fuel-costs.html), Lucy Handley, the same day, and [Cruise Industry News](https://cruiseindustrynews.com/cruise-news/2026/10/villa-vie-statement-no-outcome-is-assured/), 27 Oct, both track that statement.

The photo is the lead image on the AP story. The caption says it is the MV Odyssey at Sihanoukville Autonomous Port on Wednesday, 28 Oct 2026, and it credits Heng Sinith. This post does not describe the other vessels in the frame.

## What did the company say?

The release gives a fuel figure on a per-cabin basis. Monthly fuel that historically averaged about $1,800 has recently reached an estimated $6,000 to $8,000 in parts of Asia.

The company says it cannot pass increases of that scale on to residents. It says the shortfall put the current operating model under unsustainable financial pressure. It says that since the conflict began in late February it slowed the itinerary and spent more time in port. It says those steps did not offset the fuel increase, and that it has been unable to continue carrying the operating losses.

The release says the company is working with residents to coordinate disembarkation and temporary living arrangements. It says the next step is restructuring and recapitalization over 60 days. It says that work is ongoing, and that no outcome is assured. It says no financing or transaction has been finalized. It says further updates will come as material developments occur.

The release does not print a refund, a credit, a cabin buyback, or a date to get back on the ship. It does not name Sihanoukville. It does not name Iran, and it does not print 28 Feb.

Cruise Industry News, 27 Oct, prints the same fuel range, the same 60-day line, and the same "no outcome is assured." It does not add a refund.

CNBC shortens the fuel line to $1,800 to as much as $8,000, and it says 344 percent is CNBC's own math. The release prints a range, $6,000 to $8,000, not a single $8,000. Use the range.

## Who is on the ship?

AP says Cambodian officials put 203 passengers on the ship, mostly Americans, plus 156 crew. AP says capacity is 650. AP's headline says 200 passengers. The release prints no head count. CNBC also says the ship can carry up to 650. CNBC does not print 203.

AP's lede says hundreds of passengers had to disembark. The release says the company is coordinating that disembarkation. It does not say the crew left. CNBC says the Tuesday release covers disembarking passengers and crew. That is CNBC's reading. The release text this post uses does not say the crew got off.

## What are residents told?

The company's public instruction is the release. It is coordinating disembarkation. It says it is working to identify temporary housing. It says the restructuring over the next 60 days has no assured outcome.

AP says passengers described a different handoff. They said they got seven hours' notice on Tuesday to disembark, received no support, and had to make their own arrangements. AP says the company could not immediately be contacted for comment.

Lynn Montoya, a passenger AP names, said residents were asked again and again for money for fuel, including a fresh call to raise fuel money to leave Cambodia. She also said crew were not paid. Carol Larson told AP she bought her cabin for $120,000, paid in installments, plus monthly fees, under a plan that had promised up to 15 years aboard.

Those sentences are what those residents told AP. They are not a finding about the company's books, and they are not a refund rule. The release does not confirm the extra fuel calls, the crew pay line, or Larson's purchase price.

## When does the ship leave?

AP quotes Long Dimanche, deputy governor of Preah Sihanouk province. He said the ship arrived last Friday, was scheduled to depart Wednesday, and is now expected to refuel and leave Cambodia on Saturday. AP says the operator has not publicly confirmed any date.

The AP story is dated Wednesday, 28 Oct 2026. On that calendar, last Friday is 23 Oct and Saturday is 31 Oct. AP does not print those two dates. Do not treat them as a company schedule.

CNBC says the ship was due at Phu Quoc later this week, then India, South Africa, Brazil, and Australia, before Gibraltar in August 2030. That is the route CNBC prints. The company release does not print those ports, and it does not confirm a departure.

## How long was the voyage, and what did a cabin cost?

AP says this cruise began in October 2024 in Belfast, after a refit, and was originally intended to last 3 1/2 years. The same story's lede calls it a three-year voyage that set sail two years ago. A later paragraph says a second cruise had been tentatively scheduled to end in August 2030. This page does not collapse 3 years and 3 1/2 years into one number.

AP says the ship was built in 1993 and bought by Villa Vie Residences in 2023. AP says the operator had offered a suite for the lifetime of the vessel starting around $230,000. CNBC says rooms cost up to $11,000 a month. A purchase price and a monthly rent are different products. The release prints neither number. Larson's $120,000 is her account to AP, not the release.

AP says the company tied the fuel rise to the war in Iran. The release says "the conflict" began in late February, and it does not name Iran. CNBC writes "since the Iran war began on Feb. 28" as the company's account. Feb. 28 is in CNBC. It is not in the release. AP also writes that traffic has been curtailed in the Strait of Hormuz. That sentence is AP's. It is not a quote from the release.

## What this means for you

If you are on the Odyssey, or you paid to live on it, the document the company published is short. They say they are coordinating disembarkation. They say they are looking for temporary housing. They say the next 60 days of restructuring come with no assured outcome. They do not say what happens to a cabin purchase, a monthly fee, or a fare.

AP's passengers describe seven hours' notice and their own next step. Until Villa Vie prints a refund, do not assume one. If you are deciding on any long stay where the ship is also the place you live, the papers from 27 and 28 Oct are the risk in front of you. The voyage stopped. The money question is still open in the statement. Read the [27 Oct release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/villa-vie-residences-suspends-operations-in-cambodia-as-it-pursues-restructuring-and-recapitalization-302920622.html) before you treat a headline as a payout.
