Brilliant Lady is going to Hawaii once. [Virgin Voyages' release](https://www.virginvoyages.com/press/latest-releases/uncharted), datelined Miami on 17 Nov 2026, says the cruise is 18 nights, round trip from Los Angeles, on 11-29 Mar 2028. Bookings open 1 Dec 2026. The release calls that day Travel Tuesday.

The photo is on [Seatrade's Uncharted article](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/hotel-operations-entertainment/hawaii-new-restaurant-wellness-focus-ahead-for-virgin-voyages) the same day. The credit line is Virgin Voyages. The caption says Kauai will be among the destinations on the first Hawaii cruise in 2028. The file name is Kauai-Hawaii-Virgin-Voyages-photo.jpg. The frame is a green cliff coast and turquoise water. No ship is in it. The article does not name a photographer.

## What you can book, and when

The release calls this a one-time sailing, and Brilliant Lady's first Hawaii cruise. [The Ahoy recap](https://www.virginvoyages.com/ahoy/stories/uncharted) says the same, one-time-only, and says the vacation starts when you step on board in Los Angeles. [Virgin's Hawaii page](https://www.virginvoyages.com/ahoy/stories/hawaii-cruise), read 18 Nov 2026, also calls it a one-time sailing and puts the departure on 11 Mar 2028.

Nothing is on sale yet. The release, the Ahoy recap, the Hawaii page, and [Cruise Industry News](https://cruiseindustrynews.com/cruise-news/2026/11/virgin-voyages-details-resilient-lady-glow-up-loyalty-only-london-club/) all put the booking open on 1 Dec 2026. Seatrade's article does not print that date. [Travel Weekly UK](https://travelweekly.co.uk/news/virgin-voyages-to-make-hawaii-debut-in-2028), Ella Sagar, 17 Nov, does.

Cruise Industry News prints the return as 29 Mar 2028, which is the end date in the release's "11-29 Mar" line. The Hawaii page does not print a return date. An 18-night cruise that leaves on 11 Mar ends on 29 Mar. That math is the release's date range. It is not a port calendar.

## The ports the release names

The release names these calls:

- Hilo and Kona, on the Island of Hawaii.
- Kahului, on Maui.
- Nāwiliwili, on Kauai.
- An overnight in Honolulu, so there is time on Oahu after dark.
- Ensenada, which the release says rounds out the journey.
- 10 sea days.

The release does not print a day-by-day. Do not treat the list above as the order of the cruise. The Ahoy recap lists the same calls in a different order: Hilo, Kona, an overnight in Honolulu, Kahului, Nāwiliwili, and Ensenada. Seatrade and Travel Weekly UK name the same harbors and do not number the days either.

The Hawaii page's fast facts say six days exploring four islands, six ports of call, an overnight in Honolulu, and 10 sea days across two Pacific crossings. It names Hawaii Island, Oahu, Maui, and Kauai. It does not name Hilo, Kona, Kahului, Nāwiliwili, or Ensenada. If you are matching a booking page in December, use the release's harbor list, not only the island names.

## The sea days

The release says the 10 sea days are for unhurried time on the ship, and that the vacation starts in Los Angeles, before the islands. The Ahoy recap splits those 10 days as five out and five home, and says the programming on every sea day is shaped with Native Hawaiian people. That split, and that line about Native Hawaiian people, are on the recap. They are not in the release. The Hawaii page says the 10 sea days are across two Pacific crossings, and that the onboard entertainment is curated for this voyage. It does not print the five-and-five split.

## The $2,700 figure

The Ahoy recap prints one price: "Cabins start from as low as $2,700 per Sailor," with an asterisk. The page does not say what the asterisk means. It does not print taxes, a cabin name, or a per-night breakdown next to that figure.

The press release does not print a fare. The Hawaii page does not print a fare. Cruise Industry News, Seatrade, and Travel Weekly UK do not print a fare. Bookings are closed until 1 Dec 2026, so $2,700 is the recap's starting figure, not a cabin priced on a live booking page.
