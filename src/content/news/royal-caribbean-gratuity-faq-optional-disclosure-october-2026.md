Royal Caribbean's [gratuity FAQ](https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/onboard-service-gratuity-expense), read 23 Oct 2026, says the 18 percent on drinks, dining, room service, and the minibar is optional. You can ask Guest Relations to adjust it or remove it. Do that before the morning you get off the ship. After the sailing ends, the page says you cannot.

The daily rates on that same page are unchanged. A suite is $21.00 per guest, per day. Every other stateroom is $18.50. This is the FAQ wording, plus a booking-flow disclosure in a 21 Oct decision summary. No Royal Caribbean press release for it turned up in this check.

The photo on this page is the lead image on [Royal Caribbean Blog's 19 Oct post](https://www.royalcaribbeanblog.com/2026/10/19/royal-caribbean-gratuity-change-rumor). It shows a cocktail bar along a promenade. The image file does not name a photographer or a ship. The article text retrieved for this check did not include a caption credit.

## What the FAQ says you can change

The policy page opens with two sentences. "Gratuities are optional. Guests may adjust or remove them entirely at their sole discretion, as described below."

It then adds a percent to some purchases.

| Purchase | Added |
|---|---|
| Beverages and beverage packages | 18 percent |
| Specialty dining and specialty dining packages | 18 percent |
| Room service and the minibar | 18 percent |
| Spa and salon | 20 percent |

The next sentence is the one to keep. "These gratuities are optional and may be adjusted or removed at the guest's sole discretion."

The how-to is the sentence after that. "Guests may modify or remove any gratuity by visiting the Guest Relations desk onboard at any time prior to the morning of disembarkation. Modifications cannot be made once the sailing has ended."

That "any gratuity" line is the page's. It sits under both the daily amount and the percent charges. If you are in a group booking, the page says to check with the group leader or the travel agent.

A footnote says the daily amounts do not apply where the law prohibits them. The beverage-package FAQ has the same kind of legal limit on the 18 percent.

## Modified on one page, removed on the other

The [beverage-package FAQ](https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/beverage-package-gratuities-policy), read 23 Oct, says the gratuity is included. An 18 percent gratuity is added to a beverage package. That covers a purchase before the cruise or onboard. The law footnote is there too.

Its other footnote says this. "Gratuities are optional and may be modified at the guest's sole discretion by visiting the Guest Relations desk onboard at any time prior to the morning of disembarkation."

The verb on that page is modified. The policy page says adjusted or removed. The beverage FAQ does not use the word removed. The deadline matches. The action word does not.

## The daily amount is the same

If you did not prepay, the policy page adds this to the SeaPass account.

| Stateroom | Per guest, per day |
|---|---|
| Any suite | $21.00 |
| Every other category | $18.50 |

[Royal Caribbean gratuity rates are unchanged](/news/royal-caribbean-gratuity-rates-unchanged-october-2026/), published 22 Oct, recorded these same rates and these same FAQ sentences. Use that post for the longer rate check. It also covers a 19 Oct message, written like an announcement, that the line did not send.

[Royal Caribbean Blog](https://www.royalcaribbeanblog.com/2026/10/19/royal-caribbean-gratuity-change-rumor), Elizabeth Wright, updated 19 Oct, is the report with a spokesperson. Influencers had said the FAQ moved drinks and dining to 20 percent. The spokesperson said there has been no change to the rates. The blog quotes this. "We recently identified an error in an FAQ that incorrectly stated gratuity information. To clarify, there has been no change to our gratuity rates." The next sentence in that quote says, "Gratuities for beverages and dining remain at 18% as previously communicated. The FAQ has been corrected, and we apologize for any confusion this error may have caused."

The policy page read on 23 Oct shows 18 percent on drinks, dining, room service, and the minibar. It shows 20 percent on the spa and salon. The spokesperson quote in that article does not mention the spa percent. The quote is one outlet. The rates you can check yourself are on the policy page.

## What the 21 Oct decision summary says

[BBB National Programs](https://bbbprograms.org/media/newsroom/decisions/royal-caribbean) published a National Advertising Division summary dated 21 Oct 2026. The case line on the page is NAD Case #7561, Royal Caribbean Group. The full decision text is in a paid archive. This post uses the public summary.

The summary says Royal Caribbean modified its online booking flow during the inquiry. The sentence to quote is this. "During the inquiry, Royal Caribbean modified its online booking flow to clearly and conspicuously disclose that gratuities are optional, that customers may choose whether to prepay them, and that they may modify or remove gratuities at any time prior to disembarkation."

NAD said that modified advertising is supported. It also recommended two further lines, so the optional wording is clear in the learn-more tab and on the final checkout page.

The advertiser statement, as the summary prints it, is two fragments. Royal Caribbean "thanks NAD for its careful review" and "has implemented NAD's recommendations."

This check did not open a live booking screen, a learn-more tab, or a checkout page. The summary is the source for those steps. No Royal Caribbean newsroom post was found for it. The summary does not print $18.50, $21.00, 18 percent, or 20 percent.

The summary's clock is prior to disembarkation. The FAQ's clock is prior to the morning of disembarkation. The FAQ also says you cannot make the change once the sailing has ended. On the ship, use the FAQ.

## What no source answers

No page checked here says what you get back. That gap covers the 18 percent on a beverage package or a dining package you already paid for. The pages do not name a card refund. They do not name an onboard credit. Ask the desk before the last morning.

No page checked here dates the first day the 18 percent could come off. The NAD summary describes a disclosure in the booking flow. The FAQ is the wording you can read on 23 Oct 2026. A [22 Oct post on this site](/news/royal-caribbean-gratuity-rates-unchanged-october-2026/) already recorded that FAQ. The open point is whether the option was newly stated. The other reading is that it already existed, and the pages state it. No source here settles that.

## The crew pool, in the line's words

The policy page says gratuities that are collected are pooled and applied toward the compensation of onboard crew. The list includes dining, bar and culinary staff, stateroom attendants, and hotel services. Crew rely on what is collected. The same page says the charge is optional.

## What to do

Go to the Guest Relations desk onboard. Do it before the morning of disembarkation. The FAQ says modifications cannot be made once the sailing has ended.

If you already paid for the package, ask the desk about the 18 percent. Ask whether it returns to the card or as onboard credit. Neither the FAQ nor the NAD summary says. If you are in a group, start with the group leader or the travel agent.

The daily amount is still $18.50, or $21.00 in a suite. Drinks, specialty dining, room service, and the minibar are still 18 percent on the policy page. The spa and salon are still 20 percent.
