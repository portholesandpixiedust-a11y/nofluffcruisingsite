Okaloosa County will deploy the SS United States as an artificial reef on Thursday, 12 Nov 2026, weather permitting. The [county release](https://myokaloosa.com/sites/default/files/Users/piouser/SSUSDEPLOYMENT-NOV12.pdf), dated 26 Oct 2026, says the ship is 990 feet and calls the project the world's largest artificial reef. The site is in the Gulf, about 22 nautical miles southwest of Destin-Fort Walton Beach. [Cruise Industry News](https://cruiseindustrynews.com/cruise-news/2026/10/ss-united-states-to-become-worlds-largest-artificial-reef-on-nov-12/), 28 Oct, reports that release.

The photo is the lead image on the Cruise Industry News story. The page does not name a photographer, and it does not say where the frame was taken. The name on the bow reads United States. This post does not date the photo to 12 Nov.

## When is it, and where?

The release sets deployment operations for 10 a.m. at the site on 12 Nov. The ship is to be towed from Mobile, Alabama, days before that. The proposed position in the release is 30° 07.148' N, 86° 46.755' W.

The release says the day may draw hundreds of vessels. It tells anyone on the water to follow the safety boats.

The [Destin-Fort Walton Beach overview](https://www.destinfwb.com/explore/eco-tourism/ssus/), read 28 Oct 2026, prints the same 12 Nov date. It says the plan is weather-dependent, and that dates can change. Cruise Industry News prints 12 Nov, the 10 a.m. start, the distance, and the Mobile tow. It does not print the words weather permitting. Use the county release for that caveat.

Cruise Industry News also says the ship was towed to Mobile in 2025 after nearly three decades in Philadelphia, and that the county acquired it in 2024 from the SS United States Conservancy. The 26 Oct release does not print those years. The overview page does say the county purchased the ship in 2024.

## Can you watch?

The public sendoff is the day before. Wednesday, 11 Nov 2026, at 1:30 p.m., at the Destin-Fort Walton Beach Convention Center. The release says light refreshments will be provided.

On deployment day, the watch party starts at 9 a.m. at AJ's Seafood and Oyster Bar in Destin. The release says a live feed from the site will be on the large outdoor screen. It says the live link will be posted on [thessus.com](https://thessus.com) before deployment.

Cruise Industry News reports the sendoff time and the AJ's party. It says a live link will be available before deployment. It does not print thessus.com. The overview page says a livestream link will be shared as the date approaches. It does not name the site. For the web address, use the county release.

## Can you dive it?

The release says the reef is meant to be a premier diving destination, a home for marine life, and a contribution to the local and regional fishery. It does not print a depth. It does not print the day divers can go down.

The [diving page](https://www.destinfwb.com/explore/eco-tourism/ssus/ssus-diving/) on the Destin-Fort Walton Beach site says the ship will sit in 180 feet of water, with the top deck in the 55 to 60 foot range, for certified divers. It names local operators. It does not print an opening date.

A [listing on the same site](https://www.destinfwb.com/listing/ss-united-states-artificial-reef/50926/) says the deployment depth is 180 feet and the upper reaches will sit at 57 feet. It also says the picture on that page is a rendering. The two pages do not print the same figure for the top of the wreck. The county release prints neither number. Do not book a dive date off either page until the county prints an opening day.

## What about the museum?

Chairman Trey Goodwin, in the 26 Oct release, says people will visit the ship together with the SS United States Conservancy's land-based museum. The overview page says an interactive museum is coming soon to Okaloosa County. It does not print an opening day.

The [remediation page](https://www.destinfwb.com/explore/eco-tourism/ssus/ssus-remediation-updates/) linked from the county release says both funnels, the radar mast, and the propeller were removed and stored for that museum. It also says artifacts and more than 400 portholes were kept for it. That page says the ship has been fully remediated. The overview page still says the remediation is underway. Use the remediation page for the work status. Use the 26 Oct release for the deployment date. The remediation page only says the second week of November.

The county says the ship still holds the record for the fastest ocean liner, set in 1952. Cruise Industry News calls it 1952-built. The overview page says it was first launched in 1952. This post does not turn those three lines into one.

The release says tourism revenue and partner contributions funded the project. It does not print a dollar total.

## What this means for you

If you want to see the deployment, the public event is 11 Nov at the convention center. The watch party is 9 a.m. on 12 Nov at AJ's in Destin. The county says 12 Nov is weather permitting, and the tourism overview says the date can change. Check [thessus.com](https://thessus.com) for the live link the release promised, and check the county page again before you travel.

If you want to dive the wreck, no page in this set prints the day the reef opens. The depth numbers are on the tourism pages, and they do not match at the top of the ship. Wait for the county before you book a dive on a date.

If you want the museum, Goodwin ties it to the Conservancy, and the tourism page says a museum is coming soon in the county. The 26 Oct release does not print an opening day.
