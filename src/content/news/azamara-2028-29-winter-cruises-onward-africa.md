## The season, in one block

The release's counts, matched by both news stories, are 85 cruises, four ships, 81 countries, 210 ports of call, 94 overnight stays, and more than 200 late-night departures. The window is 29 Oct 2028 through 19 May 2029.

No page used here prints a fare.

## Onward, and Africa

Azamara Onward is the ship that returns to Africa and the Indian Ocean after what the release calls a multi-year absence. The routes span South Africa, East Africa, and West Africa. The season total for that region is 27 countries.

Jamestown, Saint Helena, is on 12 Dec 2028. The release calls it one of the world's most remote island destinations and mentions Napoleon Bonaparte's exile. Travel Weekly and Cruise Industry News print the date and the island. They do not need the exile line for you to find the call.

The Vanilla Islands in the release are the Seychelles, Mauritius, Réunion, and Madagascar. Both news stories print that list.

## Journey, Pursuit, and Quest

Azamara Journey covers the Mediterranean, Iberia, and the Canary Islands. That is 16 countries and 65 ports. Select itineraries have three days in a row in Alexandria, with time for Cairo and Luxor. The release adds that Luxor is home to the Valley of the Kings. The two news stories name Cairo and Luxor and do not print the Valley of the Kings.

The other Journey calls in all three accounts are an overnight in Marseille over Christmas Eve and an overnight in Çanakkale, Turkey.

Azamara Pursuit is the Asia-Pacific ship in the release. The Kimberley Coast in Western Australia includes an overnight in Broome. A Sydney-to-Singapore cruise includes several Great Barrier Reef gateway ports. The release does not name those reef ports. From late March through May 2029 there are five Japan Intensive sailings in a row, built around cherry blossoms. Cruise Industry News prints the Kimberley Coast, the Sydney-to-Singapore reef gateways, and the five Japan sailings. It does not print Broome. Travel Weekly says Pursuit combines Australia and New Zealand with Japan, and it stops there.

Azamara Quest, before the 2029 World Cruise, has Mediterranean, transatlantic, and Caribbean sailings from 29 Oct 2028 through 1 Jan 2029. The release calls the close a Panama Canal holiday and New Year's sailing. Travel Weekly says a Panama Canal passage. The length of the world cruise is not in this release. [The 195-night booking](/news/azamara-quest-195-night-2029-world-cruise-bookings-open/) is a separate page, already on this site. Cruise Industry News calls that world cruise 195 days. This note does not merge 195 days with 195 nights.

Cruise Industry News also says the Mediterranean, Australia and New Zealand, and Japan cherry-blossom cruises are on all four ships. The release assigns Africa to Onward, the Mediterranean sheet above to Journey, Asia-Pacific to Pursuit, and the pre-world-cruise set to Quest. It then says that, across the fleet, guests can also experience the Mediterranean, Australia and New Zealand, and Japan. Use the ship names when you book. The four-ship sentence in Cruise Industry News is that article's.

## The bonus, and the two names on the quote

The release prints a 30 percent Early Booking Bonus on every stateroom category. Combination cruises and Grand Voyages can join itineraries. The same release says the bonus is not available on Grand Voyages reservations. It does not print an end date.

Cruise Industry News prints the 30 percent Early Booking Bonus and does not print the Grand Voyages exclusion. Travel Weekly calls the offer a 30 percent early booking discount on all accommodation, and it also leaves out the exclusion. Bonus and discount are not the same word. Use the release for the rule. Use Travel Weekly for the fact that the paper says the cruises are on sale.

The release and Cruise Industry News put the quote on Mike Pawlus, head of itinerary planning. The sentences cover Africa, the Indian Ocean, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, Western Australia, and the Kimberley Coast. Travel Weekly prints those sentences and names David Siewers, international chief sales officer. This note does not decide who said them.
