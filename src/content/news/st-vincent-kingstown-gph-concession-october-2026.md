Global Ports Holding will develop and operate the cruise port in Kingstown, St. Vincent, under a 30-year concession. The company's [release](https://www.globalportsholding.com/whats-new-insights/global-ports-holding-expands-caribbean-network-with-st-vincent-cruise-port/), dated 28 Oct 2026, says operations are expected in the first quarter of 2027. That start still depends on conditions in the contract.

The photo is the group picture on that release. The banner behind the group says Signing Ceremony. The page does not name the people in the frame. [Seatrade Cruise News](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/ports-destinations/st-vincent-ties-the-knot-with-global-ports-holding), Anne Kalosh, 28 Oct, shows a different frame and captions Prime Minister Godwin Friday shaking hands with Mehmet Kutman. This post uses the GPH file, not that handshake.

## What was signed

The concession is with the Government of St. Vincent and the Grenadines. It covers St. Vincent Cruise Port in Kingstown, the capital. GPH says it follows a memorandum of understanding from June 2026.

The signing ceremony was on 26 Oct 2026 at Kingstown Cruise Ship Berth. The release says Prime Minister Dr. Godwin Friday was there, with senior ministers and other officials. [Cruise Industry News](https://cruiseindustrynews.com/cruise-news/2026/10/gph-secures-30-year-concession-for-st-vincent-cruise-port/), 28 Oct, prints the same day and the same berth.

GPH expects to start operating the port in the first quarter of 2027. Seatrade and Cruise Industry News print that quarter. All three say the start is subject to conditions in the agreement. None of them prints the day the operator changes, or a list of those conditions.

## What you will see, and when

The release gives one season figure. The port is expected to welcome over 250,000 cruise passengers in the 2026/27 season. Seatrade says more than 250,000. Cruise Industry News says over 250,000 for the 2026-27 season. Those are the same count in different words. The figure is a port forecast. It is not a count of empty cabins on your ship.

GPH plans the work in phases. The list is modernising the port, expanding the berth so larger cruise ships can dock, and improving the passenger experience. It also says the company will work with the government on shore excursions and local businesses. Mike Maura, Jr., GPH's regional director for the Americas, points at the company's ports in San Juan and Nassau as the experience it is bringing. The release does not say Kingstown will copy either of those piers.

Your ticket is unchanged by this page. No ship is named. No cruise line is named. No arrival time moves.

If your Kingstown call is this season, before the new operator starts, the release does not give you a new terminal, a new tender rule, or a new excursion menu. If your call is after the first quarter of 2027, the operator is expected to be GPH, and only if those contract conditions are met. The berth expansion does not have a start date here. Do not plan on a bigger ship, or a new pier building, from this announcement alone.

## What you should do

If Kingstown is on your current booking, keep the pier plan in your app. This concession does not cancel the call.

If you are booking a 2027 or later cruise that lists St. Vincent, ask the line which pier you will use and whether the call is still tendered or docked. The 28 Oct release does not answer that. It also prints no fare change and no construction bill. Facts above follow Global Ports Holding's release dated 28 Oct 2026, Seatrade the same day, and Cruise Industry News the same day.
