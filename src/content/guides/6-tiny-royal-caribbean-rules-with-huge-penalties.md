## 1. Smart glasses (0:50)

In February 2026, Royal Caribbean added smart glasses to its prohibited items page. You can bring them on board. The limit is where you use them.

The restricted-area list, as the script printed it from the live page on 21 Sep 2026:

> the casino; spa service areas; restrooms; locker rooms; medical facilities; security screening locations; youth facilities; during back-of-house tours; crew areas; or any location where there is a reasonable expectation of guest or crew privacy.

Wearing them on the pool deck or at dinner is fine, the video says. Using them in a place on that list is the violation.

The penalty is confiscation. Royal Caribbean's page says:

> Use in restricted areas is a violation of this policy and may result in confiscation.

[Royal Caribbean Blog, 27 Feb 2026](https://www.royalcaribbeanblog.com/2026/02/27/royal-caribbean-overhauled-its-list-of-banned-things-you-cant-bring-cruise-ship), reports a blunter line: "Smart glasses users who are seen violating this policy will have the glasses confiscated."

If those glasses carry your prescription, a confiscation is your eyesight for the rest of the cruise. The video says the page tells you to pack a backup pair that is not smart enabled. [Royal Caribbean Blog's 11 Feb 2026 report](https://www.royalcaribbeanblog.com/2026/02/11/royal-caribbean-smart-glasses-ban) says the list told prescription wearers to bring that backup. The [prohibited items page](https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/prohibited-items-onboard-policy) retrieved on 27 Oct 2026 did not include a backup sentence. It still says smart glasses may be brought onboard, and it still prints the confiscation line and the list above. Pack the backup anyway.

The policy got wider after it first appeared. That 11 Feb report describes a January notice that went further. Recording-capable smart glasses were not permitted on board at all. The chief security officer and the captain could confiscate them. The script's 21 Sep re-read found that wording gone, and the 27 Oct retrieval did too. Treat the January notice as superseded.

The 27 Feb report says Royal Caribbean rewrote the page at the end of February. The script's sourcing of that report says the restricted-area list grew from four categories to nine. Cruise Critic (17 Feb 2026) and PAX News (19 Feb 2026) reported the February change. Read the prohibited items page again before every cruise. The wider pier list is in [7 Royal Caribbean Items Security Takes at the Pier](/guides/royal-caribbean-items-security-takes-at-pier/).

**Takeaway:** Bring the glasses if you want. Keep them out of every place on that list. Pack a non-smart backup when the pair is your prescription.

## 2. Specialty dining no-shows (2:05)

Since 1 Sep 2025, Royal Caribbean charges a fee when you cancel a specialty restaurant inside 24 hours, or when you do not show up.

The fee is $25 per person. At the premium venues it is $50 per person. The script's re-read names them: Empire Supper Club, Lincoln Supper Club, Omakase, Royal Railway, Izumi hibachi and teppanyaki, and Chef's Table. The Celebration Table fee is half the cost of the experience.

A couple who misses hibachi pays $100 and eats nothing. Four people who miss Empire Supper Club pay $200. That is 2 times $50, and 4 times $50.

Royal Caribbean's cancellation page, quoted in the script:

> A $25.00 USD per person fee will apply if you cancel within 24 hours or do not show up.

> This applies to all specialty dining reservations of any kind excluding Star Class reservations.

A second Royal Caribbean wording does not match that page. When the fee launched, the company gave a statement to the trade press. The script quotes it:

> A 24-hour cancellation period will be enforced for all specialty dining reservations and culinary experiences, excluding Star Class and Dining Package reservations.

The live page drops any dining-package exception. The September 2025 statement keeps one. Both sentences are Royal Caribbean's. PAX News, Travel Weekly UK, Travel Gossip, Cruise Industry News, and Cruise Hive carried the statement in September 2025. On 27 Oct 2026 the [cancellation page](https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/specialty-dining-restaurant-cancellation-policy) still showed the quotes above, the $50 venues, and Celebration Table at half the cost. It also says a modification is not allowed inside 24 hours. The conflict with the September statement was still there.

If you bought the Unlimited Dining Package, check the reservation confirmation. Ask at the venue before you assume the fee skips you.

Everyone else can move the booking. The script quotes the page:

> You can change or cancel your specialty dining reservation in My Royal Cruise on our website at any time prior to 24 hours before your reservation time.

The video points to the app, up to 24 hours before the table. Cancel the night before when plans change.

What the table costs before a no-show fee is the next video, [What Royal Caribbean Dining Really Costs Now](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j36viJZi2kU). The write-up on this site is [3 Royal Caribbean Menu Changes That Cost You Extra](/guides/royal-caribbean-menu-changes-that-cost-extra/).

**Takeaway:** Cancel or move the reservation the night before. If a dining package is on the booking, confirm the fee before you skip the table.

## 3. Smoking or vaping on your balcony (3:25)

Smoking and vaping are out inside a cabin and on a cabin balcony. The script quotes the smoking FAQ:

> Smoking/vaping is not permitted inside any stateroom and any stateroom balcony.

One violation puts a $250 cleaning fee on your SeaPass account. A whole cigarette and one puff on a vape sit under the same line. The script quotes:

> If a guest is in violation of this stateroom policy, a cleaning fee of $250 USD will be applied to their SeaPass® account.

On 27 Oct 2026 the [smoking FAQ](https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/onboard-smoking-policy) joins those two lines. After "SeaPass® account" the sentence adds that you may be subject to further actions, and a later line points to page 2 of the Guest Conduct Policy. The $250 is the floor. The ship decides what follows.

You can smoke in designated outdoor areas. On most sailings the casino has a smoking section. That same 27 Oct page says Australia and UK sailings have no casino smoking. China and Hong Kong sailings have no non-smoking casino area.

**Takeaway:** Keep the cigarette and the vape off the balcony. Use a designated outdoor area. Check the casino rule for the country you sail from.

## 4. The pier cutoff (5:10)

You have to be checked in and on board 90 minutes before the port stops boarding. The measure is that closing time. It varies by port. On 27 Oct 2026 the port-arrival FAQ still cited Port of Vancouver at 5:00 p.m.

Royal Caribbean's page says:

> All guests must be fully checked in and onboard the ship no later than 90 minutes prior to port operations closing, or you will not be permitted to sail.

Show up at the arrival time on your boarding pass. The video says a late arrival can mean waiting for a later group. The page retrieved on 27 Oct 2026 says an early arrival does not start boarding either.

The same page encourages check-in in the Royal App at least 3 days before the cruise. The script's sourcing pass had called that 3-day step required. The page says encouraged. If the app check-in is not done, you must finish it at the pier at least two hours before the published sailing time.

Picture a 4 p.m. sailing. Skip the app, and the pier deadline is 2 p.m.

Miss either deadline and the page says you will not be permitted to sail. A few minutes of lateness costs the cruise.

Catching the ship at the next port is on you. The missed-departure FAQ, quoted in the script, says:

> Otherwise, you are responsible for any expense incurred to meet the ship at its next port of call.

That bill is flights, hotels, and meals. The option depends on your situation and your documents. You work it out with Royal Caribbean's Emergency Travel Team. The page points you to travel protection for covered delays. It promises no fare refund.

Matthew gives the same advice to everyone on this rule. Fly in the day before.

The pages are [port arrival time](https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/cruise-ship-port-arrival-time) and [missed departure](https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/miss-departure-time).

**Takeaway:** Finish check-in in the app. Be at the pier for your arrival window with time left. Fly in the day before.

## 5. One package, every adult (6:30)

One adult in the cabin buys the Deluxe Beverage Package. Every other adult of legal drinking age in that cabin has to buy it too. Royal Caribbean's FAQ says so. The script quotes:

> Any guest wishing to purchase an alcoholic beverage package must do so for all guests of legal drinking age in the same stateroom.

The same page, on the 21 Sep 2026 re-read, also says:

> only one beverage per guest will be served.

> All packages are for single guest use and cannot be shared by multiple people.

On 27 Oct 2026 that FAQ still showed the all-adults sentence. The page retrieved that day did not mention an accommodation, an exemption, pregnancy, a disability, or a medical condition.

The rule is the Deluxe package, the one with alcohol. Royal Caribbean Blog has reported that the Refreshment package and the soda package do not carry the same requirement. The script tags that carve-out as a single source.

## The verdict (9:35)

Look at the six together and the pattern in the script is this. Royal Caribbean writes each penalty for the worst case it has seen, then applies that penalty across the board.
