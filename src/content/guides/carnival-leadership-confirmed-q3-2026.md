Carnival's leadership confirmed six things this year that change how you book. Two came from the earnings call on 29 Sep 2026. The other four come from Carnival's own calls, slides, and fine print from this year. That call added new numbers to all four.

Carnival Corporation is the parent company. It owns Carnival Cruise Line, Princess, Holland America, and several other lines. CEO Josh Weinstein and CFO David Bernstein run the quarterly calls. Christine Duffy is president of Carnival Cruise Line.

This companion walks the six points in the video's order. Figures that appear in a Carnival release were checked against that release. Call quotes below were read in the [Benzinga transcript of the 29 Sep 2026 call](https://www.benzinga.com/news/26/09/62054158/carnival-reports-q3-2026-results-full-earnings-call-transcript). The same quarter is written up in [Carnival Corp's 2027 cruises are half booked at record prices](/news/carnival-corp-q3-2026-half-booked-record-prices/). More Carnival coverage is on the [Carnival news hub](/news/carnival/).

The video opens with a guess. Between now and the end of 2029, how many brand-new ships will the whole company add, counting every brand? The answer is in section 4.

Watch the video above for the spoken rundown. The sections below are the lines you can check yourself.

## 1. 2027 is half booked, and Carnival is managing it for price

On the 29 Sep call, Weinstein said Carnival is already half booked for 2027, "with both occupancy and pricing at record levels." The [same day's release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/815097/000081509726000104/a20263qearningsrelease8-k.htm) says both booked occupancy and pricing for 2027 are at record levels. The third-quarter presentation says half of 2027 is already on the books.

Last year's release shows why the comparison matters. On 29 Sep 2025, Weinstein said 2026 was "nearly half" booked, "in line with" 2025 record levels at the same time the year before, "but now at historical high prices." A year ago, the share of cabins sold was in line. The prices were the part that had moved up.

This year, UBS analyst Robin Farley asked whether "record" meant higher. The 2027 line in the release did not say higher. The 2028 line did. Weinstein answered, "No, no, '27 is higher." The rest of that sentence is garbled in the public transcript, so this page does not quote it. The video reads the answer as a bigger share of 2027 sold than 2026 had at this point, and at higher prices.

Customer deposits back the booked position. That is money already paid toward future cruises. The release puts them at a third-quarter record of $7.6 billion, $0.5 billion above last year's record. Last year's release called that record $7.1 billion. The balance-sheet table in that filing prints $7,146 million. Bernstein said 2027 capacity grows by half a percent versus 2026.

Weinstein then told analysts what Carnival plans to do with the head start. Demand is growing faster than the company is adding ships. In his words, "we have an opportunity to keep managing the booking curve for price." The video reads that as Carnival holding the cabins it still has for a higher fare. That sentence is a plain-English reading. It is not a quote.

You have already seen the move. The Middle East conflict hit Mediterranean bookings in the spring. In the [23 Jun 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/815097/000081509726000086/a20262qearningsrelease8-k.htm), Weinstein said Carnival used the occupancy advantage it had built "to deliberately prioritize pricing integrity." The video describes that as lowering Europe occupancy expectations instead of cutting fares. The specific "couple of points" line is from the June call, as the video quotes it. The release is the sentence you can read today.

There is one soft spot. Bernstein said he still expects the first quarter of 2027 to "reflect residual impacts" from that spring disruption. Bookings for that quarter have improved over the past three months. Carnival's fiscal year starts in December, because this release covers the quarter ended 31 Aug 2026. Fiscal Q1 2027 is December 2026 through February 2027. Leadership still calls that the weaker window.

For your next cruise, price the 2027 date you want while it is still open. Waiting for a broad sale means betting against the plan Weinstein described. That, too, is a reading of his words, not a second quote. If your sailing falls in December, January, or February, that is the window Bernstein still ties to the spring disruption. The release does not publish a sale for it. The longer write-up of the yields and the deposit total is the [half-booked news post](/news/carnival-corp-q3-2026-half-booked-record-prices/).

## 2. Europe ties the Caribbean for the first time

In 2027, Europe ties the Caribbean as Carnival Corporation's biggest region. Weinstein said, "Europe will for the first time tie with the Caribbean as our largest deployment region, each representing 34% of our mix." The presentation's capacity table prints the same 34 and 34. The figure covers all of the company's brands.

Carnival Cruise Line stays put in the Caribbean. On the call, Weinstein said the line has been the leader there and that he expects that to continue. The growth in the mix is heading north. He pointed to demand for "cooler weather destinations and outdoor activities," including the Norwegian fjords, the Baltic states, and Iceland.

The Caribbean side matters more if you sail Carnival Cruise Line. Competitors are adding a lot of ships there. Weinstein put the growth at "something like 37% growth over a three year period." He then answered his own question. "Does it face pressure when there's that kind of capacity coming at one time? Yeah, it does." The video cites Seatrade Cruise News for reading that 37 percent as competitors' Caribbean growth. The words in the transcript are his estimate of the growth, set against a no-growth Caribbean he said would be easier.

For your next cruise, this is the item that can help you shop. A fjord, Baltic, or Iceland sailing is taking a larger share of the company's ships. A Caribbean sailing sits in a region where more ships are chasing the same weeks. More ships in that region give you more choice when you compare fares.

## 3. Carnival now pays you for spending

Carnival Cruise Line now pays you for spending money with it. On 1 Sep 2026, Carnival switched on Carnival Rewards and retired VIFP, the old program built on nights at sea.

The new math is in the [launch release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/carnival-cruise-line-launches-carnival-rewards-302866347.html). You earn 3 status stars for every dollar on eligible Carnival purchases, and gratuities count. Gold takes 10,000 stars. That is $3,333 if every one of those dollars earns stars. Platinum takes 50,000 stars, or $16,667. Diamond takes 100,000 stars, or $33,333. The video rounds those to about $3,300, about $16,700, and about $33,300. Status is earned in a two-year window. The first window is longer. It runs from 1 Sep 2026 through 31 Dec 2028.

Christine Duffy explained the shift. "Cruise loyalty has traditionally been built around how often guests sail, and Carnival Rewards was designed to go beyond that."

The video also quotes Weinstein, from the 27 Mar 2026 call, on guests "engaging earlier in the vacation journey, purchasing more inclusive packages, excursions, and other experiences before they even step on board." Carnival's [rewards earn page](https://www.carnival.com/carnival-rewards/earn) says a prepaid drink package earns points. So do Carnival shore excursions. The company wants the money before you sail. The new program pays you for handing it over.

The 29 Sep call was the first read on how it is going. Weinstein said "thousands of members have already redeemed tens of millions of points" since the launch. Bernstein explained the accounting. "We defer a portion of revenue equal to the value of benefits earned." Part of what you pay sits on Carnival's books as something it owes you until you redeem. He also said he expects the program to be "cash flow positive from launch." The video's line that Carnival makes money on the program from day one is a reading of that sentence.

One more line is in the ticket contract. Section 8(m), read on the live page on 4 Oct 2026, says your old points and status "have been superseded and do not constitute vested rights." You cannot claim the old program as something Carnival owes you. If you had already reached Diamond under VIFP before the switch, Carnival let you keep it for life. Carnival's own [15 Sep 2025 release](https://www.carnival-news.com/2025/09/15/carnival-announces-enhancements-to-new-carnival-rewardsTM-loyalty-program-for-diamond-and-platinum-guests) sets that published cutoff at 31 May 2026. The video says "before the switch" so the point does not depend on a later argument about the date. Everyone else starts over on the new rules. The wider loyalty comparison is in [Is Cruise Line Loyalty Costing You a Better Vacation?](/guides/cruise-line-loyalty-costing-better-vacation/).

For your next Carnival cruise, open Carnival Rewards before you add a drink package or a shore excursion. Those dollars can move your stars. If you were stacking nights for a pin you no longer use, the contract says the old status is not a debt. Diamond-for-life is the exception, and only if you had already reached it before the switch.

## 4. Every new ship through 2029 is a Carnival ship

The guess from the open of the video: three.

Between now and the end of 2029, Carnival's whole company adds three new ships in the plan it has published. All three go to Carnival Cruise Line.

[Carnival Festivale](https://www.carnival.com/cruise-ships) enters service in May 2027 and sails from Port Canaveral. Weinstein said on the call that it enters service in the Caribbean in May, in time for summer. Carnival Tropicale follows in 2028 from Galveston. Then comes Carnival Destiny in summer 2029, the first ship in the Ace class. Carnival named it on [10 Jul 2026](https://www.carnival-news.com/2026/07/10/carnival-unveils-carnival-destiny-as-its-newest-ship-and-first-ship-in-next-generation-ace-class). Two more Ace-class ships are planned for 2031 and 2033, which is past this guess.

Carnival Corporation ordered that class on 23 Jul 2024. The [order release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/carnival-corporation-orders-three-additional-ships-for-carnival-cruise-line-introducing-a-new-class-of-ship-that-will-deliver-even-more-fun-to-the-most-guests-at-sea-302204077.html) described ships of nearly 230,000 gross tons, carrying almost 8,000 guests at full capacity. In that announcement, Weinstein called Carnival Cruise Line "our highest-returning brand," which is why it gets the new ships. The same release listed one delivery in each of 2025, 2027, 2028, 2029, 2031, and 2033. From here, the years still inside 2029 are 2027, 2028, and 2029. Those size figures are the order's language. Carnival has not published a final spec sheet. The video says the Q2 2026 slides put the pace at one to two new ships a year, down from three to four. The one-to-two rate is already in the 2024 order release.

On 29 Sep, Bernstein put next year's capacity growth at half a percent. Weinstein called capacity growth "pretty fixed for the next half decade." The older ships stick around, and Carnival plans to make them over. AIDA is one of its German lines. The video quotes Weinstein, from the 27 Mar 2026 call, saying "an 18-year-old ship can look and feel like a one-year-old ship." Holland America got its own program this spring. The video says it starts with Oosterdam in fall 2027. The 29 Sep release names Zuiderdam as the second Evolution ship.

In June, the video says, Weinstein told investors to expect more of these programs for more brands. On 29 Sep he named them. AIDA and Holland America get more ships next year. Cunard's Queen Mary 2 gets a major upgrade. The presentation lists that midlife work. Carnival Cruise Line is not on that list.

For your next cruise, a brand-new ship through 2029 means Carnival Cruise Line. Festivale if you want Port Canaveral in May 2027. Tropicale if you want Galveston in 2028. Destiny if you can wait until summer 2029. If you sail an older Carnival Cruise Line ship, the upgrade programs named on this call were the other brands. That announcement is the one still to wait for.

## 5. Fuel does not set your fare. Demand does.

The price of fuel has little to do with the price of your Carnival cruise. Fuel got expensive this year. The 23 Jun 2026 release says fuel costs ran nearly 30 percent higher.

On the March call, the video quotes Weinstein answering an analyst who asked whether fuel moves the company's prices. He said, "the price of fuel is somewhat irrelevant." Then, "we price as much as the market can bear." Then, "If they were willing to pay $10 more because of fuel, they should just be willing to pay $10 more." If oil prices fall, do not wait for your fare to drop with them. Carnival sets the fare by what people will pay.

On 29 Sep, another analyst asked whether Carnival would start hedging fuel. Hedging means paying to lock in fuel prices ahead of time. Weinstein called it "a short-term band-aid that sometimes pays off, sometimes it doesn't." His answer is to burn less. He said Carnival's consumption rate is down 26 percent since 2019. Bernstein put the same figure on fuel consumption per available lower berth day since 2019.

There is good news in the fine print. The [Carnival Cruise Line ticket contract](https://www.carnival.com/about-carnival/legal-notice/ticket-contract), read on 4 Oct 2026, has no fuel supplement clause. A full-page search found no mention of fuel, barrel, or surcharge. Some coverage this spring said an older contract allowed a fuel charge of up to $9 per person a day. [Cruise.Blog described that clause in March 2026](https://cruise.blog/2026/03/could-cruise-passengers-have-to-pay-fuel-surcharges). The version on Carnival's site now does not mention fuel at all. Section 8(a) does let Carnival pass through higher government taxes and fees after you have paid. The same subsection lets Carnival increase published fares and air supplements without prior notice. If you refuse the tax increase, the contract says that refusal may be treated as your cancellation. This page is a reading of that contract. It is not legal advice.

For your next cruise, do not time the booking to the oil headline. Ask whether a government tax line on your invoice has changed since you paid. Keep the confirmation. A late tax charge you cannot pay can be treated as your cancellation.

## 6. Carnival's private destinations are getting busier

Carnival is packing more people into its private destinations. Celebration Key, on Grand Bahama, welcomed almost 2.5 million guests in its first year. That count is in the 29 Sep release. On the call, Weinstein said it expects about 3.5 million next year, "with 31 ships calling," up from 26 this year. RelaxAway at Half Moon Cay welcomed more than 250,000 guests since its recent upgrade. So did Isla Tropicale in Roatán, the old Mahogany Bay. The release says "more than." The call said "approximately." This page uses the release. Next year, Weinstein said, 35 percent of Carnival Cruise Line's Caribbean capacity will visit both Celebration Key and RelaxAway on the same cruise.

An analyst asked about a second phase at Celebration Key. Weinstein said he hopes to talk about "what's phase two for Celebration Key," then added, "But it's premature to talk about that." The video cites a Q1 2026 presentation headline for this section: "Further Monetizing Our Destination Portfolio." At Celebration Key, Carnival owns the beach and sells you the cabana.

For your next cruise, look up how many ships are in port before you pay for a cabana. Four ships and more than 13,000 people is the pier's own ceiling. If your 2027 Carnival Caribbean cruise is one of the 35 percent, you get Celebration Key and RelaxAway on the same week. Price the cabana against that crowd, not against an empty beach.

## What you can do

If a 2027 cruise on a Carnival Corporation line is still open, price the date now. Half the year is booked, and Weinstein said the company plans to manage the booking curve for price. December through February is the window Bernstein still flags.

If you sail Carnival Cruise Line, open Carnival Rewards and read Section 8(m) before you count on an old tier. If you want a new ship, the next three deliveries are Festivale, Tropicale, and Destiny. If you are waiting on oil to cut your fare, the contract you can read today does not give Carnival a fuel line. It does give them a tax line.
