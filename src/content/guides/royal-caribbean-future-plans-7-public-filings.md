Royal tells you about a new idea in a press release. The lawyers file first. Trademarks, patents, and design drawings land in public files in the United States and Europe. You can read them. You do not need a law degree to do it.

This page follows the video's seven filings, in order. Each section says what the paper is, the filing or status date, and what it could mean on your cruise.

A filing is a plan on paper. Royal Caribbean has not said any of this is coming to your ship.

The owner on the trademark files is Royal Caribbean Cruises Ltd. The video notes that this is the parent of Royal Caribbean, Celebrity, and Silversea. The papers do not pick a brand.

Statuses in this guide are as of October 2026. The USPTO pages cited below were generated on 31 Oct 2026.

## What each filing can do (1:06)

A trademark protects a name. Atlas, Passport, Aura, and the loyalty slogan were filed on an intent-to-use basis. That is a sworn plan to use the name. The office accepts the oath to start the file. Before the name can register, Royal has to show the name in real use. The video explains the clock that follows approval. Royal gets six months to start using the name. Extensions can stretch that to three years. Your sailing stays the same until the name shows up on something you can book.

Co-Captain uses a different basis. Its U.S. file leans on a European registration. More on that in section 1.

A patent protects an idea. An examiner grants it or sends it back. A grant gives Royal the right to keep others off the idea. Building it is a separate decision.

A design registration protects a look. It is drawings, with almost no words.

## 1. An AI trip planner: Atlas, Passport, and Co-Captain (2:15)

Both cover software and an app that use artificial intelligence to plan leisure travel and cruise itineraries. Both cover recommendations, destination information, and itinerary guidance. The live files also cover booking a cruise on a website and in an app, and software that plans, curates, and personalizes a trip from your preferences.

The video says the two original descriptions matched word for word. The live Atlas and Passport identifications are close. A few words differ, including Passport's "travel route planning." Passport's wording is the one on the file after Royal answered an office action.

On 26 May 2026 the trademark office mailed a non-final office action on Atlas. The video says that letter could not be opened. The letter is public. It refuses Atlas for now, for a likelihood of confusion with ATLASGURU, ATLAS OCEAN VOYAGES, and ATLAS OBSCURA. Passport got an office action the same day. Royal answered on 12 Aug 2026. The office approved it for publication on 13 Aug. It was published for opposition on 8 Sep 2026. As of October 2026, TSDR still lists it as published for opposition. Publication opens a window for someone else to object. Registration comes later, and only after Royal shows use.

On 2 Sep 2026 Royal filed a third name. The mark on the form is COCAPTAIN, serial [50086521](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/statusview/sn50086521). You would say Co-Captain. ## 5. A crew cabin you will not sleep in (7:45)

On 6 Jan 2026 the U.S. patent office reissued a patent on crew cabins. The reissue is [US RE50,729](https://patents.google.com/patent/USRE50729E1/en). It corrects patent 11,383,796. Royal first filed on 8 Jul 2020. The original patent issued on 12 Jul 2022. Royal filed the reissue on 11 Jul 2024, one day before that patent turned two years old. The video notes the two-year window for broader claims in a reissue. The dates on the grant match that account.

The patent states a height problem. An industry-standard crew cabin is held to about 2.1 meters, roughly 6 feet 11 inches. Stack two bunks in that height and at least one person cannot sit up. Royal's fix puts the bunks in an L. The patent says a person up to 6 feet tall can sit up in either bunk. A staircase reaches the upper bunk. Storage is at least one cubic meter, about 35 cubic feet. The modules share one standard footprint, so any module can drop into either spot. The specification calls this a comfortable living space for extended time periods.

The patent does not name a ship. If the cabin is already on board, you would not see it. You do not go into crew cabins.

**What it could mean for you:** nothing in your stateroom. It could mean a bunk the crew can sit up in, and a place to put a suitcase. That is the whole patent. It is also why you cannot check it on a deck plan.

## 6. A cabin door that sees your face (9:14)

On 5 May 2026 Royal received [U.S. patent 12,620,283](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12620283B2/en), "Multifunction smart door device." It is the third U.S. patent in the family. The first, [11,170,593](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11170593B1/en), issued on 9 Nov 2021. The second, [11,954,956](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11954956B2/en), issued on 9 Apr 2024. All three grow out of an application filed on 5 May 2020.

Royal co-owns the newest patent with Assa Abloy Global Solutions AB, part of the lock maker Assa Abloy. Jay Schneider is a named inventor. He is also named on the train application in section 7.

The newest claims are specific. A camera watches the hallway outside your cabin. The door keeps profile data for the guests allowed into that cabin, and none for everyone else on board. It runs the facial recognition on the door. If your face matches, it unlocks.

The wider specification goes further. The door can work as a digital peephole. It can let crew in for service, unlock from afar, and send you a notification. Data from the doors can go to the line for anonymized foot traffic, hazard detection, and a log of who entered which cabin. The specification also describes facial recognition profiles held ashore and sent to the ship. The video reads that as a profile Royal could keep after your cruise and use as the starting point next time. That reading is per the filing as described in the video.

Google Patents lists the 2026 patent as active, with an anticipated expiration of 5 May 2040. The video's estimate of May 2040 matches that page.

Six years and three patents, and the video says you will not find this door on a Royal ship. This page did not walk the fleet to check.

**What it could mean for you:** a door that opens when it sees you. That is a kindness if you lose the key card. It is also a camera in the hall and a face profile tied to your cabin. The privacy rules are still unwritten. Wait for Royal to publish them before you treat this as the way your door will work.

## 7. A train car inside the ship (10:48)

The application is titled "Nested mixed reality voyages." It is application 18/650,315, published as [US 2025/0336159 A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20250336159A1/en). Royal filed it on 30 Apr 2024. It published on 30 Oct 2025. The office keeps most applications private for 18 months, which is why the video says you could not read it until then. Google Patents lists the application as pending.

The idea builds a second vehicle inside the first. The published example is a ship, with a railcar inside it. The windows are screens. They play footage recorded from a third vehicle, such as a real train on a real journey. The ship sails one way. The room around you plays another trip. The video quotes the filing as presenting "a fictional voyage in the secondary vehicle." That sentence is per the filing as described in the video. The claims this page re-read say the screens show pre-produced video of an outdoor environment, framed to look like a window.

The claims add the theater tricks. Your seat can shake when the footage crosses a railroad crossing. The lights cut out when the train enters a tunnel. Sound can play from one end of the car a moment before the other. A motion sensor can change the picture when you move. Jay Schneider, also on the door patents, is an inventor here too.

The examiner's letters are a different story. Google Patents does not show them. Per the filing as described in the video, the examiner sent the application back in October 2025 and again in April 2026. Royal responded in May. On 17 Jun 2026 the examiner mailed a final rejection. The video says a later look at the file showed no response from Royal. This page did not re-open that history. Statuses here are as of October 2026. A final rejection can still be answered. The video says Royal has until 17 Dec 2026 to respond if it pays for extensions. After that, the video says, the application goes abandoned.

The video says you can already ride a train venue on Utopia of the Seas and Legend of the Seas. That room is on ships today. This application is the one still stuck in the file.

**What it could mean for you:** a room that pretends to be somewhere else while the ship keeps its own course. Do not book a cruise for this version. The public record is an application, and the video's account has it stopped after a final rejection.
