Royal Caribbean's executives haven't hidden any of this. They just haven't put it in a press release. Across two 2026 earnings calls and one magazine interview, the company's own leadership confirmed five things that change what booking a Royal Caribbean cruise looks like going forward.

## Two more Icon-class ships are already locked in

Legend of the Seas, the third Icon-class ship, is in service now, sailing the Mediterranean this summer before repositioning to Fort Lauderdale for winter. What didn't get a press release: back in April 2026, Royal Caribbean Group signed off on two more ships in the same class, Icon Six and Icon Seven, with financing already committed. The company confirmed the orders again on its Q2 2026 earnings call on July 28, tying them to capital spending climbing toward $4.7 billion this year.

That puts three Icon-class ships in service or under construction, each holding up to 7,600 guests at full capacity, with a fourth already under construction for 2027 and a fifth and sixth funded behind it. Royal Caribbean's own capacity guidance has fleet capacity growing 4% in 2027, 6% in 2028, and 7% in 2029, which is acceleration rather than a one-time flex.

There's more behind it. Chief product innovation officer Jay Schneider told reporters in 2025 the company was in a "heavy ship-dreaming phase." That wasn't a throwaway line. Schneider also confirmed a seventh Oasis-class ship coming in 2028, and a new, smaller class called Discovery, with two ships under contract at a French shipyard for 2029 and 2032.

**What it means for you:** the biggest ships in the world aren't a one-off. They're the plan for the rest of the decade, running alongside a smaller class the company hasn't shown anyone yet.

## Some 2027 itineraries are already being rerouted

In that same July earnings release, Royal Caribbean flagged "a modest booking impact for select itineraries, primarily due to prolonged geopolitical activity." In plain terms, the company has [cancelled more than 20 sailings on Freedom of the Seas](/news/freedom-of-the-seas-2027-southampton/) that were scheduled for summer 2027 out of Miami, four-night Bahamas trips and nine-night runs to Aruba and Curaçao, and is redeploying the ship to Southampton instead. A company spokesperson told The Independent that "deployment planning is dynamic and regularly reviewed based on demand, capacity requirements, and broader fleet considerations." Affected guests are being offered future cruise credit or a rebooking onto Wonder of the Seas or Jewel of the Seas.

This isn't a one-off pattern, either. On the Q1 2026 call in April, Royal Caribbean had already flagged softer bookings for Mediterranean and West Coast Mexico itineraries tied to geopolitical developments at the time (those recovered within weeks). It's now happened twice in one year, on two consecutive calls, in two different regions, and it matters more because the Caribbean now makes up 57% of the company's total capacity, up 8% year over year, with Caribbean yields up 35% since 2019.

**What it means for you:** if you have a 2027 booking anywhere near a region with ongoing geopolitical tension, especially the Mediterranean or Mexico's west coast, check your booking status directly rather than waiting for an email.

## The "loyalty and technology" line on earnings calls is AI pricing

CEO Jason Liberty told Fortune in February 2026 that Royal Caribbean manages 15 million individual price points every day, and 90% of that is now fully automated. He described the company as "massive users of AI in a non-creepy way," his words. The system uses guest data to decide what to surface: a shore excursion you didn't know existed, a specialty-dining upgrade. Liberty's own framing for the goal was getting "more of your wallet," positioned as showing guests experiences they didn't know they wanted.

That cuts both ways. Dynamic, personalized pricing can mean a better deal if the system reads you as price-sensitive, but 90% automation also means no person is setting the fare you see. A model is, up to 15 million times a day, with a stated job of maximizing what Royal Caribbean earns.

Liberty also told Fortune the company stopped benchmarking itself against other cruise lines years ago. It now measures itself against Orlando, Las Vegas, and Taylor Swift concerts, on the logic that a family choosing how to spend its vacation budget is comparing a cruise to a three-day concert experience that can run $6,000. The AI reach goes deeper than pricing, too: the same predictive modeling has already [cut the fleet's food waste by 52% across 30 ships](/news/royal-caribbean-food-waste-52-percent/), beating the company's own 50% target, in part by forecasting burger demand in 15-minute windows on a given ship.

**What it means for you:** if you've watched a cabin's price shift more than once before final payment, that's not a glitch, it's the system working as designed. It's worth comparing fares across a couple of different accounts or booking windows rather than trusting the first number you see.

## Royal Beach Club Paradise Island is already beating almost everything else the company owns

The Nassau property opened December 23, 2025. On the Q4 2025 earnings call, COO Michael Bayley said that within four weeks it became the number-one-rated experience in Nassau among Royal Caribbean's own guests, and put its satisfaction score at just 0.8 of one percent behind Perfect Day at CocoCay, the company's longtime top-rated destination. Closing that gap in a single month isn't a normal ramp-up curve.

The 17-acre property is split into three zones, Chill Beach, Family Beach, and Party Cove (built around a two-story, flamingo-shaped swim-up bar), with dedicated ferries from ship to shore. It isn't free: a day pass runs $130+ per person, all-inclusive, meaning a family of four can pay $500+ for one port day on top of the cruise fare. Guests are paying it anyway, at a rate high enough to nearly match a free destination on satisfaction. Royal Caribbean has already confirmed a second location, Royal Beach Club Cozumel, for later in 2026, with Santorini and a South Pacific location (Lelepa) planned for 2027.

**What it means for you:** if Nassau has ever felt like the least exciting stop on your itinerary, this is the fix, and the company's own numbers back it up.

## Booking your dinner time no longer requires a phone call

On the Q4 2025 earnings call, Liberty talked about the goal of reducing "friction" and presenting "relevant options that add value to the guest," standard executive language that turned into a real fix. Until March 2026, changing a dining reservation after booking meant calling Royal Caribbean or going through a travel agent. The website now lets you log in, go to "manage reservation," and change your dining time yourself, with a waitlist and automatic upgrade if your preferred slot opens. If it doesn't open before your cruise, Royal Caribbean Blog's Matt Hochberg reports that asking to change at the Main Dining Room on embarkation day still works more often than not.

Royal Caribbean also added a special-occasion field in the same update. Log in under "room and guests" to flag a birthday, anniversary, honeymoon, or retirement before boarding. It doesn't guarantee anything (the company is upfront it's not a free-dessert entitlement), but it gives the crew a heads-up they tend to use.

**What it means for you:** it's a small, unannounced fix, but worth knowing about, and worth connecting to the AI pricing above. Every self-service change you make online, dining time included, is another data point feeding the same personalization and pricing system Liberty described to Fortune. Ninety percent of 2025 pre-cruise purchases happened through digital channels, by the company's own numbers.

## So which of these is good news?

The beach club numbers and the dining fix are straightforwardly useful, no caveats. The ship orders are good news if you love Icon-class ships and a shrug if you don't. The itinerary shuffling and the AI pricing are the two worth watching, because both can hit your wallet directly and neither one got a splashy announcement.

The pattern across all five: Royal Caribbean's leadership says almost everything eventually. The question is whether you're listening for it in the press release or the earnings call. Four of these five came from an earnings call or an earnings-adjacent interview; only the beach club got its own announcement. If you want to know what Royal Caribbean is planning next, the quarterly call is a better source than the press center.
