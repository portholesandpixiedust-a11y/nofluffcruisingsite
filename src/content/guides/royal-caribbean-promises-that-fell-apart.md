## 1. Icon of the Seas, the "most sustainable ship" (0:25)

**The promise.** When Icon of the Seas launched in January 2024, Royal Caribbean called it its "most sustainable ship to date." The pitch: LNG engines, engine heat recovery, the ability to plug into shore power at ports that have it, and solar panels up top.

**The receipt.** Friends of the Earth grades cruise lines on environmental practices, not marketing copy. Its 2024 report card gives Royal Caribbean International:

- **Overall:** D-minus
- **Air pollution reduction:** D-minus
- **Water quality and scrubber use:** F
- **Sewage treatment:** C

The F is about scrubbers. Twenty-three of Royal's 28 ships have them, and Friends of the Earth says scrubbers "merely convert air pollution into toxic water pollution." It also says LNG on Icon and Utopia pulled the air pollution grade down.

On LNG itself, Euronews reported concerns that the engines on ships like Icon can leak methane, which has more than 80 times the climate impact of carbon dioxide over 20 years. In the short term, that can make LNG worse for the climate than the fuel it replaced. As for the solar array in the renderings, the video says Royal's own numbers put it at less than 1% of the ship's power. Maritime consultant Dr. Tristan Smith summed it up in a widely quoted line: "Putting solar panels on ships, using LNG, these are incremental improvements."

To be fair, Royal told Euronews LNG was the most viable option when Icon was designed about 10 years earlier, and that the ship was built to adapt to other fuels later.

## 2. The "Venice (Ravenna)" cruise (1:46)

**The promise.** In 2023, Royal Caribbean's website listed a cruise as "Leaving from: Venice (Ravenna), Italy."

**The receipt.** Ravenna sits about 90 miles from Venice, more than two hours by car. A customer complained, and the UK's Advertising Standards Authority investigated.

Royal's defense: cruise ships cannot dock in Venice itself, Ravenna is the real port, and the name in brackets made that clear. It pointed to how it lists "Sicily (Messina)" on other cruises.

The ASA did not buy it. "Sicily (Messina)" correctly tells you Messina is in Sicily. "Venice (Ravenna)" does the opposite and makes Ravenna sound like part of Venice. On Oct. 25, 2023, the ASA ruled the listing misleading. The ad could not run in that form again, and Royal was told to make clear where its cruises depart from before people book. Royal had even offered to flip it to "Ravenna (Venice)." The ASA said that would still mislead.

The video says those cruises are now listed as round trip from Ravenna. That matches the ruling, but we have not checked current listings ourselves.

## 3. Diamond happy hour, from unlimited to four (2:52)

**The promise.** Before the pandemic, Crown & Anchor Diamond members drank free during the Crown Lounge's 5 to 8 p.m. happy hour.

**The receipt.** By 2024, cruise reporting described something different. TheStreet reported in April 2024 that Diamond members get four drink vouchers a day, each good for a drink up to $14. Happy hour drinks now need a voucher or a drink package. Otherwise you pay.

Matthew is careful here. Royal's own loyalty materials confirm the lounge and the 5 to 8 p.m. window still exist. What he could not find in Royal's published documents is the four-voucher cap itself. That number comes from cruise reporting. Either way, unlimited and four a day are different benefits.

## 4. Perfect Day Mexico (4:20)

**The promise.** Royal Caribbean announced Perfect Day Mexico in Mahahual on Oct. 9, 2024, with a 2027 opening. The full pitch came in May 2025: more than 200 acres, the longest lazy river in the world, the tallest waterslide tower in North and South America, opening in fall 2027. Royal said it was working in partnership with the local government and community. The video puts the price at $600 million.

**The receipt.**

- **December 2025:** Construction had not started. Permits and approvals were still being lined up. Royal Caribbean Blog reported the September 2027 estimate had been walked back to "late 2027," with no specific month or date.
- **May 2026:** Mexico's environment ministry, Semarnat, said it would not approve the original proposal. The concerns included mangroves, the reef and groundwater. Royal Caribbean Blog, citing Reuters, reported that three permits were denied, one of them for a pier. Royal withdrew its original application on May 19, 2026.
- **July 28, 2026:** On the earnings call, CEO Jason Liberty said the government's talks with community stakeholders are "expected to affect our previously planned timeline."

As of this video, there is no new opening date, and construction on the original plan has not started. Matthew calls this one the most disappointing of the five.

If you booked a 2027 western Caribbean cruise for this stop, treat it as unconfirmed until Royal announces a date.

## 5. The cruise port near Rome (5:52)

**The promise.** Royal Caribbean Group backed a €600 million cruise port at Fiumicino, near Rome's main airport. The pitch: more than a million passengers a year, a homeport running about 200 days a year, a marina with more than a thousand berths, plus a hotel, shops and public parks. Two Italian ministries signed off, Environment and Culture.

**The receipt.** Local residents and environmental groups challenged the approval. In 2026, a regional administrative court in Lazio sided with them. The project had been reviewed as a tourist marina, and the court said the cruise terminal at its center made it a commercial port, which needs a stricter approval process. The court did not cancel the project. It annulled the environmental approval, so construction cannot start until new approval comes through.

The town, the developer and both ministries have appealed to Italy's Council of State. No ruling had been reported as of mid-September 2026.

## The pattern (6:53)

Five promises across five areas: environmental marketing, advertising, loyalty perks, a private destination and a cruise port. Five outside checks: a watchdog report card, an advertising regulator, Royal's own current policy, a national environment ministry and a regional court.

Each promise became something smaller, or something later, once someone outside the company checked. That does not make every Royal Caribbean announcement fiction. It does mean an announcement is a starting point. Check the ruling, the fine print and the date before you book around it.
