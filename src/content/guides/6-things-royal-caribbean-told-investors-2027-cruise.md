## 1. 2027 bookings are ahead, at higher rates

The fare comes first.

Jason Liberty's line in the release is the one to keep. "While it is still early, 2027 bookings are pacing ahead of historical levels at higher rates."

Booking volume is up with that. Since the previous earnings call, volumes are up in the mid-teens across the major channels. Bookings for 2027 sailings are up in the high teens. On the call, Liberty said the booked position for 2026 and for 2027 is ahead of the prior year, at record rates.

He told Trey Bowers of Wells Fargo how to read the pace. "Customers are willing to pay us more in the future and today than they did yesterday."

The yield guide for the rest of 2026 is up as well. Fourth-quarter net yields are guided to 4.2 percent to 4.7 percent in constant currency, in line with the July range. The release also prints 4.4 percent to 4.9 percent as-reported. Full-year 2026 net yields are expected up about 2.1 percent in constant currency, and about 2.7 percent as-reported.

Third-quarter net yields rose 0.6 percent in constant currency, and 0.9 percent as-reported. Liberty said the 0.6 percent result sat about 60 basis points above guidance. Naftali Holtz repeated that 60 basis points. The release ties the beat to better-than-expected close-in demand in Europe and the Caribbean.

Capacity growth slows after 2026. The release expects capacity up 6.6 percent in 2026 versus 2025. For 2027, 2028, and 2029 it expects about 4.5 percent, 6.0 percent, and 6.5 percent. Those figures leave out later ship sales or new orders. Fewer new berths in 2027 leave fewer empty beds to fill. That reading lines up with the higher rates Liberty already described.

Holtz said 2027 yield growth should weight toward the second half. The first quarter faces about 200 basis points of structural headwinds, including dry-dock timing. Two hundred basis points is about two percentage points. That is the soft spot on the company's own calendar. The release publishes no sale for that quarter.

The fleet is already full. The statistics table prints third-quarter occupancy of 112.4 percent. The summary bullets round that to 112 percent. The release says a figure over 100 percent means some cabins held three or more people.

Michael Bayley went further on the big ships, answering a question about Icon of the Seas moving to Galveston. He said the line is filling these ships with 7,600 people, at a load factor of 133 percent, with scores in the 80s. Those guests, he said, are "happy to pay a good price." The 133 percent is his description of those ships. The fleet figure for the quarter remains 112.4 percent.

Holtz connected the Galveston move to Hero of the Seas. Hero launches on the seven-night Miami program. Icon of the Seas becomes the first Icon-class ship homeported in Galveston. The wider 2027 list is in [9 Royal Caribbean 2027 Announcements Most Cruisers Missed](/guides/royal-caribbean-2027-announcements-most-cruisers-missed/).

For your 2027 cruise, price the date while it is open. Holtz put the yield weight on the second half. A full fleet plus slower capacity growth leaves holiday weeks, school breaks, and summer with little reason to discount. That last line is a reading of the occupancy and the capacity plan.

## 2. Onboard revenue is growing faster than ticket revenue

Third-quarter onboard and other revenue was $1,706 million. A year earlier it was $1,502 million. That is a 13.6 percent increase. Passenger ticket revenue was $3,885 million, up from $3,637 million. That is a 6.8 percent increase.

The per-guest rates are this page's math. The denominator is passenger cruise days in the same release, 16,692,392 for the quarter. Divide $1,706 million by that count and onboard and other revenue is $102.20 per guest per day, about $102. A year earlier the same math is $97.81, so the per-guest onboard figure is up 4.5 percent. Ticket revenue over those guest days is $232.74, about $233. A year earlier it was $236.84, down 1.7 percent. Itinerary mix and currency move the ticket figure. The release still shows net yields up.

Liberty said the quarter's beat included strong onboard revenue, mainly on Europe and Caribbean sailings. Brandt Montour of Barclays asked about the consumer. Liberty said onboard spend had been much stronger than the company expected. Guests were paying even more than the company had anticipated.

The release says third-quarter pre-cruise and onboard revenue rates ran ahead of the prior year. Fourth-quarter pre-cruise revenue rates are booked ahead of last year.

The fare gets you on the ship. Specialty dining, a drinks package, excursions, Wi-Fi, and the spa sit on the onboard line. If you already plan to buy one, compare the pre-cruise price with the price on the ship. Paying before you sail sets a number you can see, and it hands the company the cash earlier.

Put an onboard number in the budget before you book. Keep the add-ons that are worth it to you. If you want a lower total, set a daily onboard ceiling and stay under it.

## 3. Perfect Day Mexico still has no new site or date

The May 2026 permit fight, and the months after it, are in [5 Royal Caribbean Promises That Fell Apart](/guides/royal-caribbean-promises-that-fell-apart/). Liberty's prepared remarks named Mexico and then stopped. "We have a great partnership with local communities and the government of Mexico, where we deliver close to a million vacations a year. While I know there is great interest in our efforts in Mahahual, we will share further progress and updates at the appropriate time or as they become available."

The million vacations describes cruises the company already runs with Mexico. He did not tie that number to a new Perfect Day headcount. He named no new site, no new date, and no new name.

## 4. Sandals is not a port of call

The signed terms are in [5 Things Royal Caribbean Just Confirmed About Sandals](/guides/royal-caribbean-sandals-five-things-confirmed/). The deal bulletin is [Royal Caribbean Group confirms 50% Sandals and Beaches stake for about $3 billion](/news/royal-caribbean-sandals-50-percent-stake-confirmed/). What follows is only what the 27 Oct call added.

The release expects the close in early 2027, subject to approvals. Liberty told an analyst the date is not fixed. He put the expectation at the end of 2026 or early 2027. Results would be reported on a one-quarter lag.

The release counts 18 resorts, with three more under development, and more than 1,000 acres of undeveloped Caribbean land. It says the partnership will keep the brands' identity, operating leadership, and Caribbean character.

Liberty said the company captures only 20 percent to 25 percent of its guests' annual vacation spending. About 30 percent of those guests, he said, took a Caribbean all-inclusive trip in the last two years. The release states the overlap more broadly. About 30 percent of group guests also take all-inclusive vacations, and a similar share of Sandals and Beaches guests also cruise.

He also put a price on the call. About $3 billion for the 50 percent stake, at 10 times forward EBITDA. EBITDA is a profit measure before interest, taxes, and some non-cash charges. The release does not print the dollar figure. The contract price lives in the Sandals guide linked above.

Loyalty benefits, he said, will flow in both directions. He did not list them. Synergy opportunities, he said, run to several hundred million dollars over time.

Holtz said the stake should be immediately accretive. He also said the contribution to 2027 earnings will be immaterial, and that it depends on the closing date. Liberty told Steve Wieczynski of Stifel the same thing about next year. Sandals, share buybacks, and debt refinancing sit outside the company's statement for 2027. He said the 2027 impact should be immaterial. The company said it will fold the stake into guidance once the deal closes.

One answer matters if you pictured a ship at the beach. Liberty restated the question as a ship calling at Sandals St. Vincent and tendering guests into the resort. He said no. "Sandals needs to be Sandals. Beaches needs to be Beaches."

Plan on a package or a loyalty link after the close. A 2027 itinerary does not gain a Sandals port from this call. The 2027 earnings line, by Holtz and Liberty, should not move in a material way because of the stake.

## 5. Loyalty is the plan to keep you in the family
